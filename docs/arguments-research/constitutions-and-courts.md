# Constitutions and courts: how wealth taxes have fared

Fact sheet for the Arguments pages "It's unconstitutional" and "Europe repealed it". Written 8 November 2026. Research only; nothing here is page copy.

## How to read this sheet

- **Opened in full** means the whole text of the court document (or the whole relevant part) was read by me, as text. **Opened in part** means a long document was read through a tool that returned only excerpts, or I searched it for the passages quoted. **Not opened** means I only saw it cited by someone else.
- Translations of quotes are mine (marked "tr."). Quotes in the original language are verbatim from the text I opened.
- No Wikipedia, no AI chat tool content. Press and law-firm pages are used only to say that something exists or was reported, and are labelled as such. Sibling research notes in the shared folder were used only as leads; every number below was re-checked against the court text unless marked.
- Where a court did **not** decide the merits, the sheet says so first. Several widely repeated claims turn out to be inhibitory or procedural rulings.

## 1. Germany

### 1.1 BVerfG, Beschluss of 22 June 1995, 2 BvL 37/91 (BVerfGE 93, 121)

- **Court:** Bundesverfassungsgericht, Second Senate. Referral by Finanzgericht Rheinland-Pfalz (5 K 2464/91, 4 Nov 1991).
- **Challenged:** § 10 Nr. 1 Vermögensteuergesetz, which put one rate on all taxable wealth, although land was still valued at 1964-based Einheitswerte while other assets were valued at current values.
- **Source opened:** the full text as published by the University of Bern's DFR database of the Court's decision (servat.unibe.ch). The Court's own site did not return the 1995 page for me (404). The page I read has the Entscheidungsformel, Leitsätze, paragraphs 1 to 80 and the dissent. I read the operative part, Rn. 42, 43, 79, 80 and the Leitsätze in full; the middle of the reasoning I read only in the passages quoted below.
- **URL:** https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html
- **Holding in plain words:** Taxing land at old values and other assets at today's values at the same rate breaches the equality clause (Art. 3(1) Basic Law). The Court did not strike the tax. It declared the rule incompatible and gave the legislature until 31 December 1996, with transition rules of up to five years allowed if a general revaluation is needed.
- **Operative part, no. 1 (verbatim):** "§ 10 Nummer 1 des Vermögensteuergesetzes … ist jedenfalls seit dem Veranlagungszeitraum 1983 in allen seinen seitherigen Fassungen mit Artikel 3 Absatz 1 des Grundgesetzes insofern unvereinbar, als er den einheitswertgebundenen Grundbesitz, dessen Bewertung der Wertentwicklung seit 1964/74 nicht mehr angepaßt worden ist, und das zu Gegenwartswerten erfaßte Vermögen mit demselben Steuersatz belastet."
  *Tr.:* Section 10 no. 1 of the Wealth Tax Act is, at least since the 1983 assessment year in all its versions, incompatible with Art. 3(1) of the Basic Law insofar as it burdens land tied to unit values, whose valuation has not followed value development since 1964/74, and wealth recorded at current values with the same rate.
- **Decisive sentence on the tax in principle (Rn. 42, verbatim):** "Auch der ruhende Bestand des Vermögens kann Anknüpfungspunkt für eine Steuerbelastung sein, wie dies insbesondere bei der Vermögensteuer und den Realsteuern der Fall ist."
  *Tr.:* The resting stock of wealth too can be a point of connection for a tax burden, as is the case in particular for the wealth tax and the real-property taxes.
  The Court adds that the Basic Law, in allocating tax revenue (Art. 106(2) and (6)), takes these taxes up "als zulässige Form des Steuerzugriffs" (tr.: as a permissible form of tax access).
- **Limits it set (same paragraph, verbatim):** "der Steuergesetzgeber [darf] nicht beliebig auf Privatvermögen zugreifen … der Berechtigte [hat] vielmehr von Verfassungs wegen einen Anspruch darauf …, daß ihm die Privatnützigkeit des Erworbenen und die Verfügungsbefugnis … jedenfalls im Kern erhalten bleiben." (Tr.: the tax legislator may not reach into private wealth arbitrarily; the owner is entitled by the constitution to keep the private usability and power of disposal at least in its core.)
- **Left open (Rn. 43, verbatim):** "Ob und inwieweit die Vermögensteuer unter anderen steuerrechtlichen Rahmenbedingungen auch als Instrument der Umverteilung eingesetzt werden könnte, bedarf hier keiner Entscheidung …". Tr.: Whether and how far the wealth tax could be used as an instrument of redistribution under other tax-law conditions needs no decision here. The Court notes the then yield was about DM 6bn, mostly from business assets, passed on to customers.
- **The "half-split" passage (Leitsatz 3, verbatim):** "Die Vermögensteuer darf zu den übrigen Steuern auf den Ertrag nur hinzutreten, soweit die steuerliche Gesamtbelastung des Sollertrages bei typisierender Betrachtung … in der Nähe einer hälftigen Teilung zwischen privater und öffentlicher Hand verbleibt." Tr.: The wealth tax may be added to the other taxes on yield only as far as the total tax burden on the assumed yield stays near a half-and-half split between private and public hands. This is a headnote, not the operative part (see 1.2).
- **What it said could be done instead (Rn. 79, verbatim):** "Der Verstoß gegen den Gleichheitssatz führt zu einer bloßen Unvereinbarkeitserklärung, weil die Gleichheitswidrigkeit nicht zu bestimmten Folgerungen zwingt, der Gesetzgeber vielmehr mehrere Möglichkeiten hat, den verfassungswidrigen Zustand zu beseitigen." Tr.: The breach of equality leads to a mere declaration of incompatibility, because it does not compel particular consequences; the legislator has several ways to remove the unconstitutional state.
- **Unanimity (Rn. 80, verbatim):** "Diese Entscheidung ist im Ergebnis einstimmig ergangen." Tr.: This decision was unanimous in the result. Judge Böckenförde dissented from the reasoning. He wrote (Rn. 96): "Tatsächlich enthält das Grundgesetz ein Verfassungsrecht der Vermögensteuer nicht." (Tr.: In fact the Basic Law contains no constitutional law of the wealth tax.) and that the wealth tax "läßt sich verfassungsrechtlich zulässig als eigenständiger Zugriff auf einen beschränkten Teil des Vermögens begreifen" (tr.: can be understood as constitutionally permissible, an independent access to a limited part of wealth).
- **Not decided:** The Court did not rule that a wealth tax is unconstitutional. It did not rule on a higher rate or on a tax designed with current values.
- **What followed (not re-checked by me; from a sibling note citing a DIW study):** the legislature did not replace the law by the deadline and the tax has not been levied since 1997. Use only with a source added later.

### 1.2 BVerfG, Beschluss of 18 January 2006, 2 BvR 2194/99 (Halbteilungsgrundsatz)

- **Court:** BVerfG, Second Senate.
- **Challenged:** an income-tax assessment for 1994 (BFH judgment of 11 Aug 1999, XI R 77/97). The complaint was that income tax plus trade tax together broke the half-split ceiling. **A wealth tax was not at issue**; the Court says the Vermögensteuer is "nicht Gegenstand dieses Verfahrens".
- **Holding:** Verfassungsbeschwerde dismissed ("Die Verfassungsbeschwerde wird zurückgewiesen").
- **Decisive sentences (Rn. 29 and 30, as returned from the Court's page):** a "Halbteilungsgrundsatz" as a constitutional ceiling on the burden "ergibt sich … weder aus dem Tenor noch aus den ihn tragenden Gründen" of the 1995 order. Tr.: does not follow either from the operative part or from the reasons supporting it. Rn. 30: the 1995 operative part "bezieht sich allein auf einen Verstoß gegen den Gleichheitssatz (Art. 3 Abs. 1 GG)" (tr.: relates only to a breach of the equality clause).
- **Opened:** the Court's own HTML page, which carries the complete decision. The quotes are from the fetch of that page; I did not compare the PDF. Rn. 29 text partly abbreviated by me with ellipsis.
- **URL:** https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2006/01/rs20060118_2bvr219499.html
- **Why it matters:** the "half rule" is often cited as a cap on wealth tax. The Court said in 2006 it was never a binding ceiling.

## 2. France

### 2.1 CC 81-133 DC, 30 December 1981 (Loi de finances 1982, impôt sur les grandes fortunes)

- **Opened:** the Court's page (partially read through the fetch tool). It declares the finance law for 1982 conform and rejects the referral by a single deputy as inadmissible. On the IGF it says the unit of taxation (the household) is "une règle adaptée" (cons. 7), and on usufruct (cons. 12) that contributory capacity lies with those who enjoy the income.
- **Use:** shows the first French wealth tax was reviewed and upheld in its structure. I could not read the full considérants on property rights; do not cite this decision for more than that.
- **URL:** https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/1981/81133DC.htm

### 2.2 CC 2010-44 QPC, 29 September 2010, Époux M. (ISF)

- **Court:** Conseil constitutionnel, on referral by the Conseil d'État.
- **Challenged:** articles 885 A, 885 E and 885 U of the Code général des impôts (ISF scope, household unit, rates), on grounds including equality before public burdens (art. 13 DDHC) and the absence of a family quotient.
- **Opened in full:** yes (considérants 1 to 15 and dispositif).
- **Holding:** the three articles "sont conformes à la Constitution".
- **Decisive sentences (verbatim):** "le législateur a entendu frapper la capacité contributive que confère la détention d'un ensemble de biens et de droits" (tr.: the legislator intended to hit the contributory capacity conferred by holding a set of goods and rights), and that art. 13 "n'implique pas que seuls les biens productifs de revenus entrent dans l'assiette de l'impôt de solidarité sur la fortune" (tr.: does not imply that only income-producing assets are in the base). Art. 13 test: the legislator's assessment "ne doit cependant pas entraîner de rupture caractérisée de l'égalité devant les charges publiques" (tr.: must not produce a characterised break in equality before public burdens).
- **What it left alone:** no family quotient is required.
- **URL:** https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2010/201044QPC.htm

### 2.3 CC 2017-758 DC, 28 December 2017 (Loi de finances 2018, impôt sur la fortune immobilière)

- **Challenged:** article 31, which replaced the ISF by the IFI (real estate only), on equality before public burdens, the ceiling in article 979 CGI, and usufruct rules.
- **Opened in part** (first 100,000 characters of a 172,000-character page).
- **Holding:** IFI upheld. On the base: the legislator created, for budget yield, a specific contribution on real-estate assets (cons. 41), limited to non-professional assets (cons. 42), on "critères objectifs et rationnels" (cons. 43). The lack of deduction of maintenance payments in the ceiling "n'est pas de nature à conférer à cette imposition un caractère confiscatoire" (cons. 86). One rule on usufruct, the second paragraph of A of IX of art. 31, was struck: the difference is justified neither by a difference of situation nor by a general-interest reason (cons. 59).
- **Use:** the Council let the legislature shrink the wealth-tax base to real estate. It did not say a broader wealth tax was unconstitutional. The same ruling shows an upper limit exists: a cap at 75% of income tied to the ceiling.
- **URL:** https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2017/2017758DC.htm

### 2.4 CC 2026-901 DC, 19 February 2026 (Loi de finances pour 2026)

- **Opened in part** (text to considérant 218, plus the dispositif; plus the Council's press release).
- **What it decided on wealth:** the Prime Minister referred articles 7, 8 and 11 (the "taxe holdings" on assets not used in an operating activity of holding companies; the Dutreil pact; apport-cession) "sans soulever aucun grief" (cons. 2). The Council found the procedure regular and did not examine them on its own motion. Press release: it "ne les a donc déclarées ni contraires, ni conformes à la Constitution".
- **Plain words:** the Council did **not** rule that the holding-company tax is constitutional or unconstitutional. A later QPC remains possible.
- **Other content:** the dispositif lists provisions struck as budget riders (seven) and two interpretive reservations (articles 144 and 179, neither on wealth).
- **CDHR (minimum tax on high incomes):** I could not find any ruling by the Conseil constitutionnel on it. The 2025 finance-law decision (2025-874 DC, 13 Feb 2025) does not mention it, and decision 2026-1189 QPC (27 Mar 2026), which a law-firm page cited as validating it, is about a tax on share buy-backs. Do not cite either for the CDHR.
- **URLs:** https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2026/2026901DC.htm and the press release https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/actualites/communique/decision-n-2026-901-dc-du-19-fevrier-2026-communique-de-presse

### 2.5 Conseil d'État opinion on the "Zucman" tax (autumn 2025) and the current debate

- **What the proposal was:** a minimum tax of 2% on net wealth above EUR 100m. A weaker variant (3% above EUR 10m) was put to the Assemblée in the 2026 budget. Both were rejected by the Assemblée on 31 Oct 2025 (reported by Public Sénat and LCP; not opened as official records).
- **The opinion:** the government asked the Conseil d'État. The minister for public accounts, Amélie de Montchalin, read extracts to the Assemblée (Public Sénat, 31 Oct 2025). Reported content: the measure "en l'état actuel de la jurisprudence" breaches equality before public burdens, and the ten-year rule for people who leave France is "incompatible avec la liberté d'établissement" (art. 49 TFEU).
- **Status:** I found no copy on conseil-etat.fr or the government's site. The Prime Minister had promised to publish it. A press outlet (Challenges, via secondary sites) reported an opinion dated 30 October 2025. **I could not read the text and cannot give a verbatim sentence.** I searched the transcripts of the Assemblée sittings of 24, 27, 30 and 31 Oct 2025 for the passage and did not find it, so the exact date and wording stay unverified.
- **Scholars' and officials' views:** the constitutionalist Jean-Pierre Camby is quoted by Public Sénat as not seeing the Conseil constitutionnel departing from the Conseil d'État. A Senate briefing note says a minimum tax without a ceiling risks failing review (reported in a search result; not opened). None of this is a court ruling.
- **No ruling exists:** the Conseil constitutionnel has not ruled on any version of the Zucman tax. The Conseil d'État's opinion is advice to the government, not a judgment.
- **URL (press, for the existence of the quote only):** https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/parlementaire/rejet-de-la-taxe-zucman-a-lassemblee-nationale-est-elle-inconstitutionnelle

## 3. Colombia

### 3.1 Sentencia C-395 de 2024 (Corte Constitucional, 19 September 2024, expediente D-15564)

- **Challenged:** articles 35 num. 5, 36 and 37 of Ley 2277 de 2022 (the permanent impuesto al patrimonio). Admitted charges: unequal treatment of foreign companies holding only shares or portfolio investments; and municipal competence over real property (arts. 287, 317). Charges on contributory capacity were rejected at admission.
- **Opened:** the Court's page. First 100,000 characters plus the resolutive part.
- **Holding:** **no merits decision.** The resolutive part: "INHIBIRSE de emitir pronunciamiento de fondo sobre los cargos formulados por los ciudadanos" for "ineptitud sustantiva de la demanda". Tr.: Refrain from ruling on the merits of the charges raised by the citizens, because the complaint was substantively inept.
- **Context:** the Court set aside res judicata against earlier rulings C-876/2002 and C-990/2004 because tax contexts differed, but on the municipal-competence charge said the claimants gave no constitutional reason to change the precedent (cons. 93). Para. recalling C-990/2004: "la inclusión de la propiedad inmueble en la base gravable del impuesto al patrimonio no viola" the municipal competence. I did not open C-876/2002 or C-990/2004.
- **URL:** https://www.corteconstitucional.gov.co/relatoria/2024/C-395-24.htm

### 3.2 Sentencia C-031 de 2025 (4 February 2025, expedientes D-15.273 and D-15.275, ponente Jorge Enrique Ibáñez Najar)

- **Challenged:** articles 35 to 39 of Ley 2277 de 2022, on confiscation, double taxation, horizontal equality (including lack of liquidity), and municipal competence over real property (art. 317).
- **Opened:** first 100,000 characters plus the resolutive part.
- **Holding:** **no merits decision.** The resolutive part: "Declararse INHIBIDA para conocer la demanda propuesta contra los artículos 35, 36, 37, 38 y 39 de la Ley 2277 de 2022 … por ineptitud sustantiva de la demanda". Tr.: Declare itself unable to hear the complaint against articles 35 to 39 … for substantive ineptitude of the complaint.
- **What it said along the way (verbatim fragments):** "la simple existencia de un impuesto sobre el patrimonio no es, por sí misma," [an unconstitutional effect], unless the tax causes "una privación absoluta o irrazonable del capital del contribuyente" (para. 108; the middle of the sentence was not returned by the tool and is omitted). Para. 112: the law's mitigating mechanisms (threshold of 72,000 UVT, partial exclusion of housing, progressive rates) "permiten mitigar el impacto del tributo sobre los sujetos pasivos". These are observations in a ruling that did not decide the merits, not holdings.
- **What it said could be done:** nothing was decided; claimants may refile with properly argued charges.
- **URL:** https://www.corteconstitucional.gov.co/relatoria/2025/C-031-25.htm
- **Unfavourable ruling check:** neither sentence struck the tax. Do not write "the Colombian Court upheld the wealth tax in 2024 or 2025". It declined to rule.

## 4. Spain

### 4.1 STC 149/2023, 7 November 2023, recurso 616-2023 (Pleno)

- **Court:** Tribunal Constitucional. **Challenger:** the Consejo de Gobierno of the Comunidad de Madrid. **Challenged:** article 3 of Ley 38/2022, which created the Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas (net wealth above EUR 3m), on legislative procedure, legal certainty, ability to pay and non-confiscation (art. 31.1 CE), and regional financial autonomy.
- **Opened in full:** yes, the BOE text (BOE-A-2023-25631), extracted as text.
- **Holding:** appeal dismissed in full ("desestimar íntegramente el recurso de inconstitucionalidad"). Vote 7 to 4: four judges (Enríquez, Arnaldo, Espejel, Tolosa) wrote a dissent (voto particular); the vote count is from the signature block and dissent list.
- **Decisive sentences (verbatim):**
  - On confiscation: "el impuesto temporal de solidaridad de las grandes fortunas es un impuesto sobre el patrimonio, que tendría efecto confiscatorio solo en caso de agotar el valor del mismo, no la renta generada por los bienes gravados". Tr.: it is a tax on wealth, and would be confiscatory only if it exhausted the value of that wealth, not the income the assets yield.
  - On regional powers: "la autonomía financiera reconocida por el art. 156.1 CE … permiten a las comunidades autónomas reducir a cero la tributación en el impuesto sobre el patrimonio … introduciendo una bonificación del 100 por 100 de la cuota; pero no les ampara para impedir que las Cortes Generales … establezcan un impuesto como el impuesto temporal de solidaridad de las grandes fortunas". Tr.: regional financial autonomy lets regions cut the wealth tax to zero by a 100% rebate, but does not allow them to prevent the national Parliament from creating a tax like the solidarity tax on great fortunes.
  - On timing: the tax is "un tributo sin periodo impositivo (instantáneo)" accruing on 31 December, so no retroactivity (it entered into force on 29 Dec 2022 and first accrued on 31 Dec 2022). The Court also says the rate must be judged by the effective rate (tax due against net wealth), not the nominal scale, and cites the expert committee's white paper on the point. Press pieces repeat an "average effective rate below 0.5%"; I searched the ruling text for that figure and did not find it, so I do not use it.
- **What it said could be done:** the State can use a separate tax, in "su propio espacio fiscal", to harmonise the taxation of wealth, even where regions have rebated the regional wealth tax to zero (citing STC 26/2015 on the bank-deposit tax).
- **URL:** https://hj.tribunalconstitucional.es/HJ/docs/BOE/BOE-A-2023-25631.pdf (also https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2023-25631)

### 4.2 Same-line rulings

## 5. Switzerland

- **Federal framework (verified):** The Federal Constitution (Fedlex consolidated text as of 3 March 2024) Art. 127: tax design must follow "die Grundsätze der Allgemeinheit und der Gleichmässigkeit der Besteuerung sowie der Grundsatz der Besteuerung nach der wirtschaftlichen Leistungsfähigkeit" (tr.: the principles of generality and uniformity of taxation and taxation according to economic capacity), "soweit es die Art der Steuer zulässt" (as far as the type of tax allows). Art. 127(3): "Die interkantonale Doppelbesteuerung ist untersagt." Art. 128(1): the Confederation may levy a direct tax only on the income of natural persons (up to 11.5%) and on the net profit of legal entities (up to 8.5%). So there is no federal wealth tax; cantons levy it.
- **BGE 106 Ia 342, Bundesgericht, II. öffentlichrechtliche Abteilung, 29 February 1980.** Staatsrechtliche Beschwerde against the canton of Aargau's wealth tax (1973/74) and income tax on wealth yield, with a valuation dispute over company shares. Dismissed.
  - **Opened in part** (the DFR page is an excerpt; E. 5 is missing from the page and there is no formal dispositif section).
  - **Regeste (verbatim):** "Die Eigentumsgarantie verwehrt es dem Gemeinwesen, durch übermässige Besteuerung die Vermögenssubstanz auszuhöhlen". Tr.: The guarantee of property bars the public body from hollowing out wealth substance by excessive taxation.
  - **Further sentences as returned:** the limit between permissible burden and confiscation cannot depend on a single figure ("Insbesondere kann nicht von einem ziffernmässig bestimmbaren Steuersatz allein abhängen"); relevant are rate, tax multiplier, base and duration ("Dauer der Massnahme"); a heavy burden is not confiscatory if it occurs for one or a few tax years, but would be "wenn die hohe Steuerbelastung zum Dauerzustand würde, was vorliegend nicht der Fall ist". Also: "Die Vermögenssteuer wird in der Regel aus dem Vermögensertrag bezahlt" (E. 5b).
  - **What it said could be done:** cantons may tax large wealth; the test is substance and duration, not a fixed rate.
  - **URL:** https://servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bge/a1106342.html
- **Not verified:** other Swiss rulings (BGE 105 Ia 134, 2C_826/2015) appear in search snippets about confiscatory wealth tax; I did not open them.

## 8. United States (one case among others)

### 8.1 The text

- Article I, §9, cl. 4 (verbatim, as quoted in Justice Thomas's dissent in Moore): "No Capitation, or other direct, Tax shall be laid, unless in Proportion to the Census or Enumeration herein before directed to be taken." Article I, §2, cl. 3 also requires that "direct Taxes shall be apportioned among the several States … according to their respective Numbers". Art. I, §8, cl. 1: other taxes must be uniform throughout the United States.
- Sixteenth Amendment (verbatim, quoted in Moore): "The Congress shall have power to lay and collect taxes on incomes, from whatever source derived, without apportionment among the several States, and without regard to any census or enumeration."

### 8.2 Pollock v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 158 U.S. 601 (20 May 1895, rehearing)

- **Challenged:** the 1894 federal income tax.
- **Opened:** the Cornell LII transcription of the opinion, first 100,000 of 226,000 characters. The Library of Congress scan of the official report could not be text-extracted. I did not verify the vote tally.
- **Holding:** Chief Justice Fuller: "taxes on the rents or income of real estate are equally direct taxes"; "taxes on personal property, or on the income of personal property, are likewise direct taxes"; the sections of the Act, "constituting one entire scheme of taxation, are necessarily invalid." Justice Harlan dissented.
- **Effect:** made a tax on income from property subject to apportionment. Overridden by the Sixteenth Amendment (1913). Moore's dissent says the Court "acknowledged that the Sixteenth Amendment had overruled Pollock's holding".
- **URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/158/601

### 8.3 Moore v. United States, 602 U.S. 572 (20 June 2024)

- **Opened in full:** the Court's slip opinion PDF (supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-800_jg6o.pdf), extracted as text.
- **Challenged:** the 2017 Mandatory Repatriation Tax, which attributes the undistributed income of a foreign corporation to American shareholders. Petitioners said it taxed unrealised sums without apportionment.
- **Holding (Kavanaugh, joined by Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson):** "the precise and narrow question that the Court addresses today is whether Congress may attribute an entity's realized and undistributed income to the entity's shareholders or partners, and then tax the shareholders or partners on their portions of that income. This Court's longstanding precedents … establish that the answer is yes." Affirmed.
- **Expressly left open (majority, footnote 2):** "our analysis today does not address the distinct issues that would be raised by (i) an attempt by Congress to tax both the entity and the shareholders or partners on the entity's undistributed income; (ii) taxes on holdings, wealth, or net worth; or (iii) taxes on appreciation."
- **Realization:** "To decide this case, we need not resolve that disagreement over realization." And: "Those are potential issues for another day, and we do not address or resolve any of those issues here."
- **What the majority records about wealth tax:** "the Government indicated that a hypothetical unapportioned tax on an individual's holdings or property (for example, on one's wealth or net worth) might be considered a tax on property, not income."
- **Concurrence in the judgment (Barrett, joined by Alito):** answers the question presented: whether the Sixteenth Amendment authorizes unapportioned taxes on unrealised sums: "The answer is straightforward: No." She writes that the Sixteenth Amendment and the Direct Tax Clause "distinguish between taxes on property, which are subject to apportionment, and taxes on income derived or realized from that property". She does not use the words "wealth tax". She concurs because the petitioners conceded that subpart F is valid.
- **Concurrence (Jackson):** history-based; she recounts that the Court long deferred to Congress on tax policy until Pollock, and quotes President Taft saying "Nothing has ever injured the prestige of the Supreme Court more." She does not address wealth tax.
- **Dissent (Thomas, joined by Gorsuch):** argues realization is constitutionally required ("Sixteenth Amendment 'income' is only realized income") and that the majority's reasoning is built on a "list of nonexistent taxes that the Court does not today bless, including a wealth tax", adding "cheap dicta will make no difference". He also writes that the majority "does not dispute" that "a tax on stock ownership must be apportioned" (dissent, part II). That is his description of the majority, not a holding.
- **Net:** no justice decided that a wealth tax is constitutional or unconstitutional. Four justices (Barrett, Alito, Thomas, Gorsuch) wrote that unrealised gains cannot be taxed without apportionment; that is about realisation, and does not by itself decide a net-worth tax.

### 8.4 How scholars read the direct-tax clause on wealth taxes

Verified from the article's own page or text unless marked.

- **Johnsen & Dellinger, "The Constitutionality of a National Wealth Tax," 93 Ind. L.J. 111 (2018).** Abstract (Indiana repository, opened): a net-worth tax should be among the options; the belief "that the U.S. Constitution effectively makes a national wealth tax impossible" is "wrong". https://www.repository.law.indiana.edu/ilj/vol93/iss1/8
- **Zhang, "The Wealth Tax: Apportionment, Federalism, and Constitutionality," 23 U. Pa. J.L. & Soc. Change 269 (2020).** Abstract (Emory repository, opened): *Hylton*'s rule of reason and the federalism rationale of *Pollock* both weigh against treating a wealth tax as a direct tax; with IRS data he estimates apportioned rates "ranging from 2% in D.C. to 40% in West Virginia". https://scholarlycommons.law.emory.edu/faculty-articles/114/
- **Brooks & Gamage, "The Indirect Tax Canon, Apportionment, and Drafting a Constitutional Wealth Tax" (draft of 27 Aug 2021; I read the SSRN/NYU draft, journal citation not checked).** Abstract (opened): courts following an "Indirect Tax Canon" should uphold wealth taxes "structured as indirect taxes—without requiring apportionment"; Congress can also add fallback provisions to apportion, and a fiscal-equalisation programme to rebate states. https://www.law.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/Brooks%20%20Gamage%20-%20Drafting%20a%20Constitutional%20Wealth%20Tax.pdf
- **Schizer & Calabresi, "Wealth Taxes Under the Constitution: An Originalist Analysis," 77 Fla. L. Rev. 1401 (2025).** Contrary view: a federal wealth tax would be a direct tax that must be apportioned. **Abstract not opened** (SSRN refused my fetch); citation and thesis come from a Florida Law Review listing and blog summaries in search results. Use the citation only with a note that the claim is "reported".
- **Not opened, cited in the Brooks–Gamage footnotes:** Ackerman, "Taxation and the Constitution," 99 Colum. L. Rev. 1 (1999); Glogower, "A Constitutional Wealth Tax," 118 Mich. L. Rev. 717 (2020); Hemel, "Taxing Wealth in an Uncertain World," 72 Nat'l Tax J. 755 (2019); Dodge, 11 J. Const. L. 839 (2009). I cannot state their claims from the text.
- **Where the dispute lies:** whether *Hylton* (1796) and the pre-*Pollock* cases limit "direct taxes" to capitations and land, or reach taxes on wealth itself.

## 9. California Proposition 40 (the 2026 Billionaire Tax Act)

### 9.1 What it is (text verified)

- **Official title (Attorney General, 26 Dec 2025, 25-0024A1):** "IMPOSES ONE-TIME TAX ON CERTAIN INDIVIDUALS AND TRUSTS. INITIATIVE CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT AND STATUTE." Summary: a one-time tax of up to 5% on taxpayers and trusts with covered assets over $1 billion; covered assets include businesses, securities, art, collectibles and IP, but exclude real property and some pensions and retirement accounts; 90% to health care, 10% to food assistance or education; exempts revenues from certain constitutional spending requirements.
- **Voter-guide title (Secretary of State):** "IMPOSES ONE-TIME TAX ON CERTAIN TAXPAYERS. INITIATIVE CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT AND STATUTE." and "Imposes 5% tax on certain taxpayers with assets over $1 billion; revenue primarily for health care." Source: voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/40/ (opened). The Legislative Analyst analysis (opened, same site, /analysis.htm): billionaires who were residents on 1 January 2026; one-time tax of 5% of net worth, due in 2027; payments may be spread over five years at a cost; real estate, pensions and retirement accounts generally excluded; 90% to public health care; "tens of billions of dollars" over several years; income-tax losses "less than $1 billion per year".
- **Text opened:** the amended initiative as hosted by the Attorney General (file 25-0024A1; cover letter dated 24 Nov 2025; 32 pages), extracted as text. **I could not open the Secretary of State's own copy of the measure text** (the voter guide text page returned 403), so I cannot confirm it matches word for word.
- **Structure as written (verbatim):** Section 50301(a): "An excise tax is imposed for tax year 2026 on the activity of sustaining excessive accumulations of wealth by applicable individuals with net worth of $1 billion dollars ($1,000,000,000) or more, and on applicable trusts." 50301(b): "the tax imposed is 5 percent of the net worth"; between $1.0bn and $1.1bn the rate falls by 0.1 percentage point for each $2m below $1.1bn. Residency is tested on 1 January 2026 (the "tax obligation date"); net worth is valued at 31 December 2026. Section 50308(n): "'Tax obligation date' means January 1, 2026." Section 50308(o): "'Valuation date' means December 31, 2026." An optional deferral account is in Section 50304.
- **Constitutional part:** new Art. XIII, Section 37, which says the Act "provides for taxation on all forms of personal property and wealth, whether tangible or intangible" and permits classification for exemption; creates a separate reserve fund; and authorises "a facial challenge" through a validation action "without requiring the payment of the tax". Section 50314: a validation action must be filed within 60 days of voter approval or the tax is "held to be facially valid"; venue Sacramento County.
- **Amendment:** Section 50310: the Legislature may amend the Act "by statute passed in each house … two-thirds of the membership concurring, if the statute is consistent with and furthers the purposes" of the Act.
- **Severability:** Section 50311(a): "While the Act has been carefully designed to comply with existing law …"; (b) courts shall preserve the tax "to the maximum extent consistent with the United States and California Constitutions", including by reforming dates.

### 9.2 Does it avoid the federal direct-tax issue?

- The Direct Tax Clauses restrict Congress. A state tax is not subject to apportionment among the States. So Prop 40 does not raise the Moore or Pollock question as such. What the text does is label the tax an excise on an activity, put a state constitutional section behind it, and set up an early validity review. I found no passage in the text saying the label was chosen to avoid the federal clause, and I do not claim the drafters' intent.
- Other federal and state constitutional arguments (due process and retroactivity from the 1 Jan 2026 residency date, Commerce Clause apportionment, equal protection, takings, state revision-versus-amendment, single subject) are listed in a commentary piece by a named author (California Globe, 13 July 2026). **These are arguments, not filed cases.** That page reports no pre-election lawsuit. I searched for pre-election challenges and found none.

### 9.3 How it qualified

- Per press accounts (CalMatters, Ballotpedia news in search results; the Secretary of State certification page not opened): signatures were submitted in late April 2026; on 17 June 2026 the Secretary of State reported at least 980,438 valid signatures, enough to qualify; the withdrawal deadline of 25 June 2026 passed and the measure stayed on the ballot as Proposition 40. Dates and the count are from press and need the Secretary of State's certificate before use in print.

## 11. Safe to write

1. In 1995 the German Federal Constitutional Court held that the wealth tax could, in principle, be a constitutional tax on "the resting stock of wealth", and struck only the uneven valuation of land against other assets, giving the legislature until the end of 1996 (operative part, Rn. 42, Rn. 79). It said the legislature had "several possibilities" to repair it.
2. The "half-split" rule is not a binding ceiling: the Court said so in 2006 (2 BvR 2194/99, Rn. 29).
3. France's Conseil constitutionnel upheld the ISF's key provisions in 2010 (2010-44 QPC) and the real-estate wealth tax (IFI) in 2017 (2017-758 DC), save one usufruct rule.
4. In February 2026 the Conseil constitutionnel was referred the holding-company tax without any complaint and said it was neither conform nor contrary (2026-901 DC).
5. The Colombian Constitutional Court twice declined to rule on challenges to the wealth tax in 2024 and 2025, finding the complaints inadequately argued. The tax remains in force.
6. Spain's Constitutional Court upheld the national solidarity tax on great fortunes in November 2023, 7 to 4, saying a wealth tax is confiscatory only if it exhausts the wealth itself and that regions cannot block the State from taxing wealth they have rebated to zero.
7. Switzerland has no federal wealth tax because the federal constitution lets the Confederation tax only income and profit; cantons levy it, and in 1980 the Federal Supreme Court said it becomes confiscatory only if it hollows out substance over time, not at a fixed rate.
8. In Moore v. United States (2024) the Supreme Court majority said in a footnote that it does not address "taxes on holdings, wealth, or net worth". No justice decided the question.
9. The US Constitution applies only to federal taxes. The apportionment rule has no bearing on a state tax (state taxes face other constitutional limits).
10. Prop 40's text calls the tax an "excise tax", sets 5%, a $1bn threshold, residency on 1 Jan 2026 and valuation on 31 Dec 2026; it was on the 3 November 2026 ballot and the result is pending as of 8 November; do not state a result or a count until the Secretary of State certifies it.
