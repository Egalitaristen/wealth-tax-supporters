# Portugal — preliminary research (30 October 2026)

## Scope and status
Seed pass planned across national actors and samples of regional and local government, in Portuguese and English. This first pass is incomplete; local and regional searches have been sampled, but no complete official-record sweep has been done.

## Evidence and leads
The [2019 Bloco programme](https://programa2019.bloco.org/capitulo-2/11-a-economia-para-toda-a-gente/164-medidas-fiscais-para-combater-abusos-e-repor-a-igualdade.html) proposes a progressive levy on total wealth. In [November 2024](https://observador.pt/2024/11/11/be-propoe-taxar-fortunas-acima-de-tres-milhoes-de-euros-e-criar-o-imposto-elon-musk/), Mariana Mortágua presented a tax on wealth above €3 million. The [LIVRE parliamentary resolution](https://observador.pt/2024/09/25/livre-quer-que-governo-apoie-iniciativas-internacionais-para-tributar-grandes-fortunas/) supports international wealth-tax initiatives. In 2025 [Causa Pública](https://observador.pt/2025/05/06/legislativas-causa-publica-defende-imposto-sobre-fortunas-e-lucros-dos-fundos-imobiliarios/) recommended an annual tax on fortunes over €2.5 million. The [Socialist parliamentary resolution](https://app.parlamento.pt/webutils/docs/doc.pdf?Inline=true&fich=9989191a-3164-4a14-b9c9-55ad03728a1f.docx&path=6148523063484d364c793968636d356c6443397a6158526c63793959566b6c4d5a5763765247396a6457316c626e527663306c7561574e7059585270646d45764f546b344f5445354d5745744d7a45324e433030595445304c574935597a6b744e5456685a44417a4e7a49345954466d4c6d52765933673d) discusses the G20 minimum and asks for an international minimum tax; it does not propose a standalone Portuguese tax. Economist José Reis is included as apparent support (grade C) because he discusses wealth thresholds but the interview does not specify the tax base. In a [2014 speech](https://www.pcp.pt/pcp-apresenta-conclus%C3%B5es-das-jornadas-parlamentares-realizadas-no-distrito-de-lisboa), João Oliveira set out the PCP proposal for a tax on multiple types of financial wealth above thresholds. French economist Thomas Piketty discussed progressive wealth taxation in a Portuguese interview, but is excluded from this country register under the rule for people living and working elsewhere.

## 2024–25 financial-wealth proposals
The PCP proposed a 0.8% annual tax on net financial assets above €1 million in [budget amendment 1805C](https://www.pcp.pt/sites/default/files/documentos/oe2025propostaalteracao1805C.pdf) (2024), signed by four deputies. Three of them signed a corresponding [2025 proposal](https://www.pcp.pt/sites/default/files/documentos/oe2026propostaalteracao215C.pdf). These are taxes on a broad financial asset class, rather than on real estate or financial transactions alone. The register does not assert that either amendment passed.

The [CGTP-IN workers’ priorities for 2025](https://www.cgtp.pt/images/images/2025/05/prioridades2025.pdf) also call for taxation of financial holdings. The confederation does not specify a rate, net basis or frequency in this document.

## Further national sources
The [2011 Bloco bill](https://app.parlamento.pt/webutils/docs/doc.pdf?Inline=true&fich=abf5d4ea-7d72-4310-a563-d6a9a9adeb7c.doc&path=6148523063484d364c793968636d356c6443397a6158526c6379395953556c4d5a5763765247396a6457316c626e527663306c7561574e7059585270646d457659574a6d4e5751305a5745744e3251334d6930304d7a45774c5745314e6a4d745a445a684f5745355957526c596a646a4c6d527659773d3d) proposes an annual net-wealth tax; official parliamentary records list Rita Calvário as a presenter. The group bill does not name individual signatories, so further sponsor attribution needs separate official evidence. Ricardo Oliveira advocated taxing shares, large deposits and other financial holdings in a [2014 PCP speech](https://www.pcp.pt/tributacao-do-capital-financeiro-do-patrimonio-mobiliario-das-transaccoes-financeiras). PAN and Inês de Sousa Real proposed [international action on large fortunes](https://www.parlamento.pt/ActividadeParlamentar/Paginas/DetalheIntervencao.aspx?BID=380939) in 2024; their resolution does not specify a tax design.

## Economist interviews, 2012 and 2021
Former finance minister [Miguel Cadilhe](https://www.occ.pt/fotos/editor2/diarioeconomicoimposto21junho.pdf) proposed a one-off tax of 4% on net assets with a relatively high exemption; he envisaged including both individuals and companies. [Susana Peralta and João Rodrigues](https://abrilabril.pt/nacional/quando-pandemia-ataca-sobretudo-quem-trabalha) discussed possible taxes on wealth or people with substantial assets. Their comments are recorded as apparent support (grade C), because neither specified an asset base or advocated a concrete design in that interview.

## Further party and parliamentary search
A [2025 campaign event](https://www.pressminho.pt/legislativas-francisco-louca-e-mariana-mortagua-dao-aula-sobre-justica-fiscal-em-guimaraes/) documented Francisco Louçã explaining support for the Bloco wealth-tax proposal. A 2011 Green Party statement calls for consideration of a tax on large fortunes; this is logged as a lead pending a clear endorsement within the research window. Earlier 2005 and 2009 parliamentary votes fall outside the register’s time window.

## Additional public voices
Historian [Raquel Varela](https://www.publico.pt/autor/raquel-varela) called for a wealth tax in a 2021 opinion article; the accessible publisher excerpt does not set out the design. Azores parliamentary candidate [Pedro Gaspar Amaral](https://sites.google.com/view/pedrogasparamaral/colabora%C3%A7%C3%B5es/di%C3%A1rio-insular) argued for a progressive tax on fortunes over approximately €3 million in a 2025 column. His geographic placement represents his Santa Maria connection; his national constituency encompassed the Azores.

## European parliamentary group
The [Greens/European Free Alliance study](https://www.greens-efa.eu/files/assets/docs/greensefa_wealth_tax_v2__interactive_.pdf) recommends a progressive net-wealth tax and models its potential effects in Portugal. The Portugal figure is an estimate, not tax revenue collected. This European Parliament group is distinct from Portugal’s Green Party; the latter remains an open lead.

## Further broad sweep
Former parliamentary leader [Pedro Filipe Soares](https://www.rtp.pt/noticias/economia/be-propoe-imposto-sobre-luxo-e-mais-valias-urbanisticas-e-pequeno-almoco-gratuito-nas-escolas_n501059) defended tax alternatives that included a large-fortune tax in 2011. [Catarina Martins](https://bloco.org/media/jornalverao2015.pdf) supported fiscal reform targeting large fortunes in a 2015 interview alongside her party’s wealth-tax platform. The [Tax Justice Network](https://taxjustice.net/press/reassert-tax-sovereignty-to-unlock-trillions-for-climate-finance/) advocates progressive wealth taxes internationally and its separate research estimates Portugal-specific revenue; this does not imply a Portugal-only campaign. An Azores party platform was sampled, but regional assembly records are not exhaustively searched.
