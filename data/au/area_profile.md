# African Union: area profile

**Scope.** The Pan-African Parliament (PAP), the African Union's legislative organ: up to 275 seats, five per member state, filled from each state's own parliament (pap.au.int/en/honorable-members-parliament). Like the European Parliament, it is not a register of its own: a PAP member who supports a wealth tax is recorded in their own country's register (data/<iso2>), and the seat lights from there. Of the 55 member states only Nigeria has a register so far, so most seats cannot light yet. The AU Assembly and Commission (governments, as in data/un) are not in scope of this run.

**Seats.** `python skill/scripts/seats.py data/au` reads the MP list of each state at pap.au.int/mps/<state> (names and committee; no party, no photo needed). 220 MPs were listed on 2026-10-26; the rest of the 275 are shown as "Not on the list" (states whose delegation is not listed, and incomplete delegations). Seats are grouped by the AU's five regions because the list gives no party.

**Run R001 (2026-10-26).** Searched for a PAP member, resolution or AU organ taking a position for an actual wealth tax: web searches in English and French; the PAP open-data portal's full text search (opendata.pap.au.int, documents to about 2020: nothing on a wealth tax; one 2019 resolution on an African model double taxation agreement and a 2023 resolution backing the EU financial transaction tax on climate action [verify], both outside the definition); the 2019 Hansard hit for "tax justice" read in full (an ATAF and Tax Justice Network Africa seminar on illicit financial flows: corporate tax, not wealth). Found: no supporter. The AU is a G20 member and joined the 2024 Rio finance ministers' language on taxing ultra-high-net-worth individuals [verify], which is the "effectively taxed" wording this project does not count (see data/un/exclusions.csv X0002). Candidates checked: South Africa's Vuyani Pambo (EFF, a PAP member; the EFF has called for a wealth tax but nothing of his own was found), Kenya's Esther Passaris (backed a capital gains submission, not a wealth tax), Zimbabwe's delegation (Zimbabwe's 2023 "wealth tax" is a 1% levy on residential property above US$250,000: a property tax, out of the definition).

**Run R002 (2026-10-26).** All 88 PDFs listed on pap.au.int/en/documents (resolutions, reports, communiques, 2019 to 2025) downloaded and searched for wealth, billionaire, super-rich and tax-the-rich wording: only incidental uses ("wealth creation"); the PAP has no document on a wealth tax. Thirty members from eight states searched by name (Kenya, Ghana, Namibia, Senegal, Zambia, Tanzania, Uganda, South Africa): none tied to a wealth tax. The AU as a body: no Assembly decision found. ATAF's April 2026 high-net-worth individuals guide and the African Group's push for stronger "effective taxation of high-net-worth individuals" wording in the UN tax convention talks (Article 6) are enforcement and cooperation, not a tax on the stock of wealth (exclusions.csv). Oxfam in Africa's 2025 report, launched ahead of the AU Mid-Year Coordination Meeting, does call for a wealth tax and is addressed to the AU: logged as lead L0017 (it is not a PAP member, so it has no seat).

**Not yet searched.** PAP plenary Hansards and committee reports after 2020 (the open-data portal stops around 2020; the 2026 documents are on pap.au.int/en/documents and need a page-by-page read); each member's own national parliament record (a wealth-tax motion or bill sponsored in Egypt, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, Zimbabwe, Namibia, Tunisia and so on); the AU Assembly decisions and the African Group's positions in the UN tax convention talks; the 15 or so states whose delegations are not listed. A blank is "not searched", never "no support".

**Leads logged** (data/leads.csv, L0011 to L0014): the EFF, Kenya's Institute of Public Finance, Akina Mama wa Afrika, Greenpeace Africa.
