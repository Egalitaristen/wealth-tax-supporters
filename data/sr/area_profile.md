## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Statutory Framework:** Suriname possesses a statutory recurring net wealth tax: the **Wet Vermogensbelasting 1944** (Gouvernementsblad 1944 no. 185; promulgated 1960 G.B. 1960 no. 85, as amended by S.B. 1980 no. 104/116, S.B. 1984 no. 106, S.B. 2002 no. 107, and S.B. 2004 no. 152). Article V(T) of [S.B. 2024 no. 168](https://www.dna.sr/media/jblpbo4t/s-b-_2024_no-_168__wet_van_5_december_2024___aanpassing_bestaande_wetgeving_in_verband_met_de_invoering_van_het_nieuwe_burgerlijk_wetboek.pdf) also amends an Article 6 household-effects reference; this does not resolve the rate conflict.
- **Tax Design:** An annual net wealth tax on residents and certain local assets of non-residents. Official sources conflict: [DNA's consolidation](https://www.dna.sr/media/dqqltkkx/vermogensbelasting_1944.pdf) states 8 per thousand (0.8%), the [tax administration's copy](https://belastingdienst.sr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Wet-Vermogensbelasting.pdf) states 3%, and the [2021 recovery plan](https://gov.sr/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Herstelplan-2020-2022-versie-10-mei-2021.pdf) mentions 3 per thousand (0.3%). Current rate and thresholds require reconciliation with original amendments.
- **Core Controversy & Debates (2010–2026):**
  - *Under-enforcement & Loophole Exploitation:* Historical accounts described very low wealth-tax receipts; the [proposed 2026 budget](https://gov.sr/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/OB-2026-FINAL.pdf) estimates SRD 20 million, which is not an actual collection figure. Vast amounts of private wealth and real estate are sheltered in legal entities—specifically private foundation structures (*stichtingen voor particulier beheer*) and holding companies—which are not subject to the 1944 act.
  - *Bracket Creep & Inflation:* Rapid currency depreciation and inflation severely eroded the real value of the statutory threshold (SRD 100,000), technically pulling ordinary homeowners into the tax net while the ultra-rich shelter their assets.
  - *2017 Parliamentary & Tax Debate:* NDP MP Amzaad Abdoel and tax consultant Roy Shyamnarain publicly criticized the Bouterse government for neglecting wealth tax collection (collecting under 5M SRD while over $1B sat in private bank accounts) while resorting to regressive fuel surcharges (*government take*).
  - *2020 Cabinet Fiscal Policy (Achaibersing):* Upon taking office in the Santokhi administration in August 2020, Minister of Finance and Planning Armand Achaibersing declared that active enforcement of the existing statutory *Wet Vermogensbelasting 1944* was an essential component of the government's financial-economic recovery package to capture domestic revenue left uncollected.
  - *2021 Legal Analysis:* Jurist Carlo Jadnanansing published a prominent analysis reminding the public that Suriname has had a wealth tax since 1944, demonstrating that the state forfeits substantial revenue by failing to enforce it and urging the closing of the real estate foundation loophole.
  - *2023 National Budget Scrutiny:* During the plenary debate on the 2023 national budget, NPS MP Patricia Etnel attacked the government for projecting a token SRD 8.1M in annual wealth tax revenue while imposing austerity and subsidy cuts on ordinary citizens. She highlighted that at least 8,000 wealthy families and commercial companies in Suriname pay virtually zero wealth tax due to non-enforcement.
  - *2024 Economic Association Analysis (VES):* In formal state consultations with a Dutch parliamentary delegation in Paramaribo (documented in Kamerstuk 36 572, nr. 1), VES Chairman Steven Debipersad identified the poor functioning of the tax office and the reality that "virtually no wealth tax is paid" as major structural impediments holding back Suriname's economic growth.
  - *2024–2025 VAT vs. Wealth Tax Clash:* During parliamentary debates on the Tax Identification Number and VAT (*BTW*), BEP leader Ronny Asabina criticized the Santokhi administration for relying heavily on consumption taxes and wage taxes instead of wealth taxation. Finance Minister Stanley Raghoebarsing opposed wealth taxation, questioning whether the solidarity principle should compel hard-working individuals to share their wealth with the less industrious. Earlier in 2022 APB debates, Asabina had highlighted that the existing 1944 statute was toothless and yielded less than $400,000, calling for either radical enforcement on the rich or shifting to easier-to-collect vehicle taxes; by 2024–2025, BEP formally integrated equitable wealth taxation into its core platform.
  - *2025 Union Proposals (Ravaksur):* Union confederation Ravaksur formally demanded in collective bargaining (April 2025) raising the wealth tax exemption threshold from SRD 100,000 to SRD 1,600,000 to protect ordinary working families. In June 2025, Ravaksur spokesperson and economist Armand Zunder proposed amending the law to encompass legal entities (foundations and holding companies), estimating an annual revenue potential of at least 500 million SRD.

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
- **De Nationale Assemblée (DNA):** Unicameral, 51 seats elected for five-year terms; the 2025 election used proportional representation in a single national constituency, replacing the ten-district system.
- Official website: https://dna.sr
- Wetgeving (legislation repository): https://dna.sr/wetgeving/
- Plenary debate agendas and minutes: https://dna.sr/het-politiek-college/vergaderingen/
- Note on transparency: DNA publishes act texts and member rosters. Plenary audio/video broadcasts are streamed on YouTube (DNA Suriname), but searchable verbatim transcripts of every sitting are not indexed; news portals (*Dagblad Suriname*, *Waterkant*, *SUN*) provide detailed verbatim parliamentary coverage.

## 4. Parties
- **BEP (Broederschap en Eenheid in de Politiek):** Party led by Ronny Asabina (2 seats in the 2020-2025 term; 1 seat won in the 2025 election). A May 2025 news report attributes a call for greater focus on wealth tax to BEP; in November 2025 Asabina called for its abolition. Whether this represents a formal change in party policy is unverified.
- **PRO (Partij voor Recht en Ontwikkeling):** Reformist party founded in 2018. In its 2024 Labour Day declaration, PRO called for a "thorough wealth tax" (*gedegen vermogensbelasting*) alongside mining revenue enforcement to tackle rising inequality.
- **NDP (Nationale Democratische Partij):** Party that held 16 seats in the 2020-2025 term and won 18 in 2025. MP Amzaad Abdoel championed wealth tax collection in DNA plenary in 2017.
- **NPS (Nationale Partij Suriname):** Parliamentary party that held 3 seats in the 2020–2025 term and won 6 in 2025. Prominent MP Patricia Etnel strongly challenged government undercollection of the wealth tax during the 2023 budget plenary debate.
- **VHP (Vooruitstrevende Hervormings Partij):** Led by former President Chan Santokhi; the party held 20 seats in 2020-2025 and won 17 in 2025. Finance Minister Stanley Raghoebarsing publicly opposed implementing or expanding wealth taxes in 2024. Faction leader Asiskumar Gajadien mentioned wealth tax in budget debates but prioritized tax exemptions for holding companies (excluded as X0003).
- **DA'91 (Democratisch Alternatief '91):** Reformist extra-parliamentary party led by Angelic del Castilho. In its 2025–2030 platform (*Uw paspoort op weg naar de nieuwe Republiek*), DA'91 proposes abolishing the traditional statutory wealth tax (Wet Vermogensbelasting 1944) due to high administrative costs and replacing it with an anti-corruption *Belasting Onverklaarde Weelde* (Unexplained Wealth Tax) targeting concealed assets (excluded as X0004).

## 5. Government below national level
- **Districts (Districten):** 10 districts (Paramaribo, Wanica, Nickerie, Coronie, Saramacca, Commewijne, Marowijne, Para, Brokopondo, Sipaliwini). Each governed by an appointed District Commissioner (*Districtscommissaris*) and an elected District Council (*Districtsraad*).
- **Resorts (Ressorten):** 62 sub-districts, electing Resort Councils (*Ressortraden*).
- **Taxation powers:** Subnational bodies have virtually no independent legislative taxing power; taxation is centrally legislated by De Nationale Assemblée and administered by the Belastingdienst Suriname. Local councils do not pass local tax motions.
- **Official source:** OCHA / HDX COD-AB Suriname gazetteer and shapefiles (valid 2019-10-24, CC BY-IGO).

## 9. Known networks
- **Ravaksur (Raad van Vakcentrales in Suriname):** National trade union umbrella confederation comprising C-47, De Moederbond, CLO, PWO, BvL/FOLS, and OSAV. Actively campaigning for wealth tax threshold adjustments and closing corporate loopholes.
- **VES (Vereniging van Economisten in Suriname):** Leading economic professional association frequently critiquing fiscal policy, regressive consumption taxation, and the underperformance of wealth taxation. Led by Steven Debipersad.
