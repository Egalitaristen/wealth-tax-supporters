# Algeria — Wealth Tax Register Profile

## Scope

- **Jurisdiction**: Algeria (`DZ`), sovereign state in North Africa (Maghreb).
- **Population & economy**: ~46.8 million (ONS 2024 estimate); hydrocarbon-led mixed economy where ordinary taxation (*fiscalité ordinaire*) has increasingly been mobilized since the 2014 oil price collapse to cover civil-service wages and social transfers.
- **Official languages**: Arabic and Tamazight; French is widely used in government administration, tax legislation (*Code des impôts directs et taxes assimilées*, CIDTA), business, and economic journalism.
- **Currency**: Algerian dinar (`DZD`, د.ج / DA). In popular and parliamentary usage, large figures are frequently expressed in *centimes* (`1 DZD = 100 centimes`):
  - `50 million DZD` = `5 milliards de centimes` (5 billion centimes, ~$373,000 USD at official exchange rates)
  - `80 million DZD` = `8 milliards de centimes` (8 billion centimes, ~$597,000 USD)
  - `100 million DZD` = `10 milliards de centimes` (10 billion centimes, ~$746,000 USD)
- **Tax authority**: *Direction générale des impôts* (`DGI`, المديرية العامة للضرائب) under the *Ministère des Finances* (وزارة المالية), operating alongside the *Direction générale du domaine national* (`DGDN`, المديرية العامة للأملاك الوطنية).

## Wealth tax history & live proposals

### 1. The 1993 *Impôt sur le patrimoine* (Articles 274–282 CIDTA)

Algeria first introduced a statutory net wealth tax in the **1993 Finance Law** (*Loi de finances pour 1993*, Legislative Decree No. 93-01 of 19 January 1993) under the title ***Impôt sur le patrimoine*** (الضريبة على الأملاك), codified in **Articles 274 to 282 of the *Code des impôts directs et taxes assimilées* (CIDTA)**.

- **Tax base**: Net value as of 1 January of real estate (built and unbuilt property, property rights) and specified high-value movable assets (private motor vehicles, yachts/pleasure boats, racehorses, aircraft, jewelry, gold/precious metals, art objects) held by natural persons domiciled in Algeria (on worldwide property) or non-residents (on Algerian-situs property), less deductible debts backed by certified legal proof.
- **Evolution of thresholds and rates (1993–2019)**:
  - Initially progressive from **0.5% to 2.5%** above an exemption threshold (raised in 2003 to 12M DZD, in 2006 to 20M DZD, in 2008 to 30M DZD, and in 2015–2016 to **100M DZD** / 10 billion centimes at rates from **0.25% to 1.5%**).
  - Under the **2016 Finance Law**, the rate schedule was temporarily compressed into a dual bracket (1% on 50M–100M DZD and 1.5% above 100M DZD), and under the **2020 initial Finance Law** (*Loi de finances pour 2020*, enacted December 2019), briefly replaced by fixed lump-sum payments (*forfait*) of 250,000 DZD to 500,000 DZD between 100M and 500M DZD and 0.5% above 500M DZD.
- **Revenue allocation**: Under Article 282 CIDTA, proceeds were originally split **60% to the State budget**, **20% to the municipal equalization fund** (*Fonds commun des collectivités locales*, FCCL / *Caisse de solidarité et de garantie des collectivités locales*, CSGCL), and **20% to the National Housing Fund** (*Fonds national du logement*, FONAL).
- **Administrative dead letter**: Throughout the 1990s, 2000s, and 2010s, the *Impôt sur le patrimoine* remained largely uncollected ("*حبر على ورق*" / ink on paper) due to the absence of a digitized national land cadastre, the prevalence of cash transactions in the informal sector (*أصحاب الشكارة* / "bag-of-cash holders"), and lack of political enforcement against politically connected oligarchs.

### 2. Parliamentary Left Campaigns & the PLF 2014 Amendment (2013)

During the oil boom and early post-boom budgets, left-wing opposition deputies repeatedly tabled Finance Bill amendments to replace the dormant 1993 property tax with an enforced annual **Impôt sur la fortune (ISF)**:

- **Parti des Travailleurs (`PT`)** (`A0001`), led by Secretary General **Louisa Hanoune** (`A0002`) and parliamentary spokesperson **Ramdane Taazibt** (`A0003`), introduced annual amendments to each Finance Bill calling for a progressive wealth tax on large fortunes and oligarchs.
- In **November 2013**, deputy **Smaïl Kouadria** (`A0004`) sponsored a formal amendment to the **2014 Finance Bill (PLF 2014)** to establish an effective wealth tax, arguing before the APN Finance Committee that the existing 1993 *Impôt sur le patrimoine* was mere "ink on paper" (*حبر على ورق*). The FLN–RND majority on the Finance Committee rejected the amendment.

### 3. The 2018 Finance Bill (*PLF 2018*) Battle (September–November 2017)

Following the collapse of hydrocarbon revenues, Prime Minister **Ahmed Ouyahia** (`A0015`) and Finance Minister **Abderrahmane Raouya** (`A0016`) introduced **Article 12 of the draft 2018 Finance Bill (*Projet de loi de finances pour 2018*)** in September 2017 to overhaul the dormant regime into an **Impôt sur la fortune**:

- **Proposed PLF 2018 schedule**: Lowered the exemption threshold from **100M DZD to 50M DZD** (5 billion centimes) and raised progressive marginal rates to **1%** (50M–100M DZD), **1.5%** (100M–200M DZD), **2%** (200M–300M DZD), **2.5%** (300M–400M DZD), and **3.5%** (above 400M DZD), accompanied by heavy penalties for non-declaration and cross-checking via municipal mayors (APC presidents).
- **Suppression by the APN Finance Committee**: After fierce lobbying by the employers' association **Forum des Chefs d'Entreprises (FCE)** (`X0002`, led by **Ali Haddad**) and businessman-deputies from the ruling FLN/RND coalition, the APN Finance and Budget Committee chaired by **Toufik Torche** (`X0001`, FLN) voted on **17 November 2017** to delete the wealth tax provisions from PLF 2018, confirmed by the plenary on **26 November 2017**.
- **Opposition defense of the ISF**: The deletion triggered an uproar across the parliamentary opposition:
  - **Parti des Travailleurs (`PT`)** (`A0001`), **Louisa Hanoune** (`A0002`), and **Ramdane Taazibt** (`A0003`) denounced the cancellation as a "*coup de force*" by the business lobby and retabled an ISF amendment in the **2019 Finance Bill (PLF 2019)** in November 2018 (again rejected by the FLN–RND majority).
  - **Mouvement de la Société pour la Paix (`MSP` / حمس)** (`A0007`), its president **Abdelmadjid Menasra** (`A0008`), and its parliamentary group leader **Nacer Hamdadouche** (`A0009`) fought in committee and plenary to retain Article 12, condemning its removal as "a clear alignment against the poor" while VAT and fuel taxes were raised on ordinary citizens.
  - **Front des Forces Socialistes (`FFS`)** (`A0005`) parliamentary group leader **Chafaâ Bouaiche** (`X0003`, deceased 2021) and Islamist coalition deputies **Tahar Habchi** (`A0011`, Ennahda) and **Lakhdar Benkhellaf** (`A0010`, El Adala) likewise publicly condemned the suppression of the wealth tax.

### 4. Enactment of the *Impôt sur la fortune* in the 2020 Supplementary Finance Law (*LFC 2020*)

Following the 2019 *Hirak* protest movement, the trade union federation **UGTA** (`A0012`) under Secretary General **Salim Labatcha** (`A0013`) demanded in October 2019 a reduction in wage income tax (IRG) paired with the institution of a wealth tax (*impôt sur la fortune*).

In **May–June 2020**, under President **Abdelmadjid Tebboune** (`A0014`) and Finance Minister **Abderrahmane Raouya** (`A0016`), the government enacted **Articles 26 to 32 of the 2020 Supplementary Finance Law (*Loi de finances complémentaire pour 2020*, Law No. 20-07 of 4 June 2020)**, formally renaming the tax in the CIDTA to ***Impôt sur la fortune (ISF)*** (الضريبة على الثروة):

- **Threshold & progressive bracket schedule (2020–2025)**:
  - Up to **100,000,000 DZD**: **0%**
  - **100,000,001 to 150,000,000 DZD**: **0.15%**
  - **150,000,001 to 250,000,000 DZD**: **0.25%**
  - **250,000,001 to 350,000,000 DZD**: **0.35%**
  - **350,000,001 to 450,000,000 DZD**: **0.50%**
  - **Above 450,000,000 DZD**: **1.00%**
- **Expanded tax base**: Extended assessment beyond real estate and luxury vehicles to cover **all movable and immovable property and rights**, including bank deposits, shares/equities, bonds, life insurance claims, and assets held abroad by Algerian tax residents, while exempting a taxpayer's **primary residence up to 450M DZD** (*abattement* / exemption rules refined in subsequent finance laws) and professional productive assets (*biens professionnels*).
- **Revenue distribution**: Modified Article 282 CIDTA to allocate **70% to the State budget** and **30% to the municipal solidarity and guarantee fund (CSGCL)**.

### 5. Digitization Drive, *CAD-Fisc*, and the 2024 Presidential Campaign (2021–2024)

Because self-declarations under the 2020 ISF remained low (yielding only ~41M DZD in 2021 across a handful of voluntary filers), enforcement became a central fiscal battleground:

- **Presidential and ministerial enforcement directives**: President **Abdelmadjid Tebboune** (`A0014`) issued formal Council of Ministers instructions in October 2021 and October 2022 ordering the digitization of the tax administration and property registries to census individual fortunes rather than taxing only salaried workers. In May 2023, Finance Minister **Laaziz Faid** (`A0017`) instructed the DGI to make 2023 "the year of the wealth tax" (*l'année de l'impôt sur la fortune*) and on **2 August 2023** presided over the signing of the **DGDN–DGI inter-agency data-sharing protocol (*CAD-Fisc*)** linking cadastral land records directly to tax assessment for the ISF.
- **2024 Presidential Election (*Vision pour demain*)**: During the September 2024 presidential election campaign, **Front des Forces Socialistes (`FFS`)** (`A0005`) candidate and First Secretary **Youcef Aouchiche** (`A0006`) made the effective enforcement and **municipal decentralization** of the *Impôt sur la fortune* to local *Assemblées populaires communales* (APCs) a signature plank of his economic programme (*Vision pour demain*).

### 6. The 2026 Finance Law Reform: Lowering the Threshold to 80M DZD (2025–2026)

To broaden the tax base and improve revenue yield, the **Ministry of Finance** (`A0018`) introduced **Article 25 of the 2026 Finance Bill (*Projet de loi de finances pour 2026*)** in October 2025, endorsed and strengthened by the **APN Finance and Budget Committee** (`A0019`):

- **Threshold lowered**: From **100,000,000 DZD** (10 billion centimes) to **80,000,000 DZD** (8 billion centimes).
- **Revised progressive rate schedule**:
  - **Below 80,000,000 DZD**: **0%**
  - **80,000,000 to 120,000,000 DZD**: **0.20%**
  - **120,000,001 to 200,000,000 DZD**: **0.30%**
  - **200,000,001 to 300,000,000 DZD**: **0.40%**
  - **300,000,001 to 400,000,000 DZD**: **0.50%**
  - **Above 400,000,000 DZD**: **1.00%**
- **Reporting & penalties**: Moved from a quadrennial declaration to an **annual declaration requirement by 31 March**, backed by a **10% late-filing surcharge** (rising to **25%** after formal notice) and expanded *contrôle fiscal* (tax audit) powers over undeclared net wealth.

## Parties

- **Parti des Travailleurs (`PT` / حزب العمال, `A0001`)**: Trotskyist / democratic-socialist party founded in 1990 and led by **Louisa Hanoune** (`A0002`), with parliamentary leaders **Ramdane Taazibt** (`A0003`), **Smaïl Kouadria** (`A0004`), **Djelloul Djoudi** (`A0026`), and **Nadia Chouitem** (`A0027`). Historically the most vocal parliamentary advocate of an annual *Impôt sur la fortune*, tabling amendments across multiple legislatures (2012–2019).
- **Front des Forces Socialistes (`FFS` / جبهة القوى الاشتراكية, `A0005`)**: Algeria's oldest opposition party (founded 1963, member of the Socialist International), led by First Secretary and Senator **Youcef Aouchiche** (`A0006`). Campaigned in the 2024 presidential election on instituting and decentralizing the *Impôt sur la fortune* to municipalities.
- **Mouvement de la Société pour la Paix (`MSP` / حركة مجتمع السلم — حمس, `A0007`)**: Largest opposition bloc in the 9th APN (65 seats), with former party president **Abdelmadjid Menasra** (`A0008`) and former parliamentary group leader **Nacer Hamdadouche** (`A0009`). Supported the retention of the *Impôt sur la fortune* during the 2017–2018 Finance Bill debates and criticized exemptions for wealthy oligarchs while wage earners bore high IRG and VAT burdens.
- **Mouvement El Bina (`حركة البناء الوطني`, `A0022`)**: Major parliamentary party (39 seats in the 9th APN) led by **Abdelkader Bengrina** (`A0023`). Pledged in its 2019 presidential election programme to broaden the tax base and enforce collection of the net wealth tax (الضريبة على الثروة) alongside wage tax relief.
- **Parti Socialiste des Travailleurs (`PST` / حزب العمال الاشتراكي, `A0024`)**: Anti-capitalist left-wing party founded in 1989; publicly condemned the government and Parliament in December 2017 for burying the *Impôt sur la fortune* while offering tax amnesties to informal-sector barons.
- **Union pour la Renaissance, la Justice et la Construction (*Union Ennahda–Adala–Bina*)**: Islamist parliamentary coalition in the 8th APN (2017–2021) whose deputies **Lakhdar Benkhellaf** (`A0010`) and **Tahar Habchi** (`A0011`) backed the *Impôt sur la fortune* and opposed its removal by the ruling coalition.
- **Front de Libération Nationale (`FLN`) & Rassemblement National Démocratique (`RND`)**: Dominant presidential-majority parties. While RND leader and Prime Minister **Ahmed Ouyahia** (`A0015`) introduced the 2017 ISF draft bill and RND deputy **Hakim Berri** (`A0028`) criticized its rejection, the FLN–RND parliamentary majority led by Finance Committee chair **Toufik Torche** (`X0001`) voted to delete the wealth tax in November 2017 under pressure from the FCE business lobby (`X0002`), before the executive later enacted the *Impôt sur la fortune* in the 2020 Supplementary Finance Law and lowered its threshold to 80M DZD in the 2026 Finance Law.

  - `X0002` **Forum des Chefs d'Entreprises (FCE) / Ali Haddad** — Algeria's main employers' organization (now *CREA*), which lobbied against the 2017 ISF proposal as a "bad signal to investors".
- **Deceased (`deceased`)**:
  - `X0003` **Chafaâ Bouaiche** (died 28 July 2021) — former FFS Parliamentary Group Leader who publicly opposed the deletion of the ISF in November 2017.
  - `X0004` **Abderrahmane Benkhalfa** (died 23 April 2021) — former Minister of Finance (2015–2016).
  - `X0005` **Karim Djoudi** (died 13 May 2022) — former Minister of Finance (2007–2014).
  - `X0007` **Achour Idir** (died 4 April 2019) — National Coordinator of the *Conseil des Lycées d'Algérie* (`CLA`, recorded as `A0025`) and spokesperson of the Autonomous Unions' *Intersyndicale*, who denounced the APN Finance Committee's cancellation of the ISF in November 2017.
- **Insufficient evidence / technical commentary only (`insufficient_evidence`)**:
  - `X0006` **Abderrahmane Mebtoul** — economist who repeatedly analyzed the ISF as "inapplicable" without a comprehensive digital information system and integration of the informal sector, without clearly endorsing the tax itself.
  - `X0008` **Soufiane Djilali** — president of *Jil Jadid*, who dismissed the 2017 ISF proposal as demagogic and uncollectible without structural economic reform.
  - `X0009` **Rassemblement pour la Culture et la Démocratie (`RCD`)** — secular opposition party whose May 2020 statement on LFC 2020 dismissed the *Impôt sur la fortune* as "populist in nature insofar as no serious registry exists to tax the rich".
  - `X0010` **Fares Mesdour** — economist who argued that a statutory wealth tax would trigger tax evasion in the absence of a property-declaration culture and advocated voluntary Islamic *zakat* funds instead.
  - `X0011` **Abdelkader Berriche** — economic analyst and former APN deputy who commented on the administrative hurdles and lack of a national property database facing the 2020 wealth tax without expressing normative support.
