# United Nations: area profile

**Scope.** The General Assembly's 193 seats, one per member state. A seat is a government, not a person. A government is recorded (actors.csv, `Government of <state>`) when it has a sourced public position for an actual wealth tax: a law it enacted, or a proposal, joint article or initiative its ministers put their names to. A seat is lit while that government is in office and its latest evidence is support; a change of government ends the position and unlights the seat.

**Design decisions** (issue 102). What counts as a state backing it: the same rules as everywhere (section 2 of the skill). A G20 or UN text that says only that the very rich should be "effectively taxed" does not count, and one ministry speaking is not the government (Germany, recorded in exclusions.csv). Seats are grouped by the UN's regional groups, from the UN's own list; the United States and Türkiye sit with WEOG. Observer states have no seat.

**Run R001 (2026-10-26).** Found: Brazil and Spain (lit: the April 2024 joint billionaire-levy article, and the July 2025 Seville initiative on taxing the super-rich), South Africa (co-signed the 2024 article, then rejected a domestic wealth tax in May 2025: not lit), Colombia (the Petro government's wealth tax, Ley 2277 of 2022 and the 5% rate of 2026: former, as the new government says it will abolish the tax). Most official pages (gov.br, hacienda.gob.es, regjeringen.no) refuse scripts, so some quotes come from the pages that reproduce them (La Moncloa, the German BMZ, the G20 Information Centre, Cape Town ETC). Replace them with the originals when read in a browser.

**Run R002 (2026-10-26).** Added Argentina (Ley 27.605, 2020) and Bolivia (Ley 1357, 2020), both former governments; Chile (the 2022 Boric reform bill, former); Norway (lit: the Støre government keeps the formuesskatt, a grade B defend-existing position). Uruguay's government rejects a tax on the richest and is in exclusions.csv, as are Brazil's UN tax convention texts (effective taxation, not a wealth tax).

**Run R003 (2026-10-26): UN records.** Read the UN's own summary of every member state's General Debate statement for the 76th to 81st sessions (gadebate.un.org, about 190 pages a session), UN Meetings Coverage pages for the Second Committee, ECOSOC and the Financing for Development conference, and UN News. Found: Cuba (foreign minister, 2024: a tax on billionaires; lit), Brazil's President Lula in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (grades C, C, B), Spain's foreign minister at the Seville conference, and UN News's own report of the Spain-Brazil initiative. Nothing turned up in the 2021, 2022 or 2026 summaries. The summaries are short and written by the UN, so a state that said it in passing is missed; the verbatim records (A/80/PV.5 and so on) are PDFs and are not searched. press.un.org and gadebate.un.org refuse scripts, so `wtdb.py checkquotes` cannot read them: the quotes from those two sites were read in a browser, and the two UN News quotes pass the check.

**Run R004: verbatim records.** The A/PV and A/C.2 PDFs were not read: the UN document server answers scripts with a bot check, which this run did not get around. The 70 Second Committee coverage pages (2022 to 2026) held no statement from a state for a wealth tax.

**Not yet searched.** Switzerland, France's government on the taxe Zucman, Mexico, Belgium, the Netherlands, Kenya, Nigeria, Barbados, Indonesia, and statements in the General Assembly itself. A blank is "not searched", never "no support".

## Review corrections

Eight evidence rows were removed because their quote does not name a wealth tax or only names a document (a G20 communiqué, a joint text saying the super-rich should be 'taxed more efficiently', speeches asking for 'proportional' or 'minimal global' taxation, and a raised threshold for Norway's existing tax). Brazil keeps the April 2024 joint call for a global levy on billionaires and its 2025 General Assembly speech on a minimum global tax; Spain keeps the joint call and its 2025 statement on minimum taxation of large fortunes; Norway keeps Labour's pledge to keep the wealth tax. All four lit seats stay lit.
