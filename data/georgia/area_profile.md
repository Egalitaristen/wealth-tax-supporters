Five local councils were sampled through exact-phrase web searches in R003; their records were not systematically searched.
- **Languages:** English, Georgian, Russian.

## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **No wealth tax exists.** Georgia has a flat 20% income tax, a property tax on real estate and vehicles above an income threshold, and no inheritance tax. The 2010 Transparency International tax overview says there was no wealth, inheritance or capital gains tax.
- **2018:** On.ge reported that Labour presidential candidate Shalva Natelashvili advocated a French-model wealth tax in Georgia. The same report mentions large businesses, but does not give a tax base, rate or threshold; its linked candidate questionnaire timed out. This is recorded as named support, not as a verified formal party policy: https://on.ge/story/29220-%E1%83%A1%E1%83%98%E1%83%9B%E1%83%93%E1%83%98%E1%83%93%E1%83%A0%E1%83%98%E1%83%A1-%E1%83%92%E1%83%90%E1%83%93%E1%83%90%E1%83%A1%E1%83%90%E1%83%AE%E1%83%90%E1%83%93%E1%83%98-%E1%83%9C%E1%83%90%E1%83%99%E1%83%9A%E1%83%94%E1%83%91%E1%83%98-%E1%83%91%E1%83%98%E1%83%A3%E1%83%A0%E1%83%9D%E1%83%99%E1%83%A0%E1%83%90%E1%83%A2%E1%83%98%E1%83%90-%E1%83%93%E1%83%90-%E1%83%A1%E1%83%AE%E1%83%95%E1%83%90-%E1%83%A0%E1%83%90-%E1%83%AE%E1%83%94%E1%83%93%E1%83%95%E1%83%90-%E1%83%90%E1%83%A5%E1%83%95%E1%83%97-%E1%83%A1%E1%83%90%E1%83%9E%E1%83%A0%E1%83%94%E1%83%96%E1%83%98%E1%83%93%E1%83%94%E1%83%9C%E1%83%A2%E1%83%9D-%E1%83%99%E1%83%90%E1%83%9C%E1%83%93%E1%83%98%E1%83%93%E1%83%90%E1%83%A2%E1%83%94%E1%83%91%E1%83%A1-%E1%83%94%E1%83%99%E1%83%9D%E1%83%9C%E1%83%9D%E1%83%9B%E1%83%98%E1%83%99%E1%83%90%E1%83%A1%E1%83%90-%E1%83%93%E1%83%90-%E1%83%93%E1%83%90%E1%83%A1%E1%83%90%E1%83%A5%E1%83%9B%E1%83%94%E1%83%91%E1%83%90%E1%83%96%E1%83%94.
- **2003:** the Zhvania government proposed a tax on expensive houses, holiday homes and cars. This is before 2010 and was described as a tax on specified property, not established net wealth (`X0005`; https://civil.ge/ka/archives/131239).
- **2017 and 2023:** the Social-Democrats for the Development of Georgia and a list of 25 organisations, including the Georgian Trade Unions Confederation, asked to delete article 94 of the constitution, which bars tax rises without a referendum. The coverage frames it as opening the way to progressive income tax; no text found mentions wealth (`X0001` to `X0003`).
- **2024 and 2026:** in 2026 the income threshold for exemption from property tax on non-land assets rose from GEL 40,000 to 100,000 (`X0004`); in 2024 Georgian Dream passed relief for offshore assets brought to Georgia (`X0006`; https://civil.ge/archives/600399); opposition MPs criticised the second as protecting Bidzina Ivanishvili. Nobody called for a wealth tax.
- **Election of 26 October 2024:** the CEC count gave Georgian Dream 89 of 150 seats; the four opposition lists boycotted or lost their mandates.

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
See `skill/references/records/ge.md`. No bill, motion or speech on a wealth tax was found.

## 4. Parties
Georgian Dream: tax cuts and the Estonian model for company profits. United National Movement and the Free Democrats: tax cuts (ISET comparison of the 2020 platforms). Labour Party: its 2018 presidential candidate proposed a named wealth tax according to On.ge; no party policy document was verified. Social-Democrats for the Development of Georgia: article 94, progressive income tax. None meets the definition on the evidence found.

## 5. Government below national level
Regions, Adjara's autonomous republic, Tbilisi and municipalities; taxation of wealth is a national power and the only local tax of note is the property tax. R003 exact-phrase Georgian web searches for the councils of Tbilisi, Batumi, Kutaisi, Rustavi and Gori found no verified supporters. This limited sample is not a search of complete council records; no broad sweep is justified yet.

## 8. Coverage notes
- **Touched:** parties, unions (GTUC), left movements, economists in the press, civil society, polls.
- **Election Compass:** The 2020 ISET blog described a wealth-tax issue while grouping party positions. The researchers’ 2021 report lists all 30 questions and party scores: the relevant rich-tax question asks about a larger share of income, not a net-wealth tax (https://iset-pi.ge/storage/media/other/2022-04-08/cf7e3800-b741-11ec-913d-8f8f7d0b305d.pdf, pp. 35–36). The 2024 Compass report likewise asks about income (https://library.fes.de/pdf-files/bueros/georgien/22098.pdf). Neither establishes a wealth-tax retraction by Natelashvili or a qualifying party endorsement. The original platform remained inaccessible (D0007).
- **R002:** a candidate statement was found in the Georgian-language campaign comparison. Generic advocacy of taxing the rich in Tornike Chivadze’s 2019 essay was not counted because the stock of net wealth is not clearly the tax base. A blank means not found, not no support.
- **R005:** The full 2018 *Striving for Shared Prosperity* report models progressive personal income-tax rates, not a net-wealth tax (X0009; https://www.academia.edu/38234807/Progressive_Taxation_Georgia). A secondary account of GTUC’s 2026–2029 programme lists progressive taxation without identifying the tax base; the original action-plan page returned 403 (D0009).
- **R006 civil society:** Read the GTUC 2026–2029 action plan (D0009, X0010) and Social Justice Center article on social-benefit wealth filters (X0011). The first discusses progressive taxes and wage income-tax relief; the second discusses means tests and income-tax credits. Neither establishes wealth-tax support. Sampled Transparency International Georgia, FactCheck Georgia, Solidarity Network and Heinrich Böll Foundation publications; an independent union, a foundation-supported research network and a social-democracy movement remain leads (D0010–D0013). An anonymous-upload manifesto has an ambiguous corporate tax base and unverified provenance, not a verified supporter.
- **Polls:** none on a wealth tax found.
- **Seats:** the chart shows list totals from the 2024 count, not verified current composition. Natelashvili held a 2020–2022 mandate and is not a current member; his seat is not lit.
- **Map:** OCHA COD-AB (CC BY-IGO); Tbilisi, Abkhazia and the Provisional Administration have no level-2 below them and are drawn as units.
