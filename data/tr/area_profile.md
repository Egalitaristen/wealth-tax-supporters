## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Historical context:** Turkey enacted the *Varlık Vergisi* in 1942 (Law No. 4305), an emergency one-off capital levy during WWII that was applied with severe discriminatory criteria against non-Muslim minorities and repealed in 1944. Modern economists, unions, and parties explicitly distinguish their proposals as an annual, progressive net wealth tax (*artan oranlı servet vergisi*) grounded in human rights and democratic wealth distribution.
- **Live legislative bills:**
  - **Bill 2/2613 (2024-10-16):** Submitted by Mersin MP Perihan Koca (DEM Parti / TÖP), titled *"Servet Vergisi ve Temel Gelir Güvencesi Kanun Teklifi"* ([TBMM Record](https://www.tbmm.gov.tr/Denetim/Kanun-Teklifleri)). Proposes an annual progressive net wealth tax of 2% to 10% on fortunes exceeding 40 million TL, with revenue earmarked for a universal basic income guarantee (*Temel Gelir Güvencesi*).
  - **Budget Interventions:** In November 2019, HDP MP Garo Paylan delivered a comprehensive plenary address on the 2020 Central Budget demanding an explicit progressive wealth tax on total assets ([TBMM Tutanak](https://www.tbmm.gov.tr/Tutanaklar/TutanakMetinleri)).
- **Crises and Debates (2020–2024):**
  - **COVID-19 pandemic (2020–2021):** Trade unions (DİSK, KESK) and socialist economists demanded an emergency wealth tax on corporate and financial fortunes to fund social safety nets.
  - **Kahramanmaraş Earthquakes (February 2023):** Prompted major debates on funding reconstruction. Prominent billionaire industrialist İbrahim Erdemoğlu (Erdemoğlu Holding / SASA) publicly called in March 2024 for a mandatory 1% wealth contribution on all assets over 6 million TL ([Ekonomim](https://www.ekonomim.com/kose-yazisi/varligi-6-milyon-liranin-uzerinde-olandann-yuzde-1-servet-vergisi-alinsin/734260)).
  - **Fiscal Stabilization and G20 Poll (June 2024):** During Treasury & Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek's summer 2024 tax reform package, the Earth4All / Ipsos G20 public opinion survey found that **78%** of adults in Turkey support a wealth tax on the rich—the second highest level in the G20 ([Earth4All Survey](https://earth4all.life/global-survey-2024/), [İklim Haber](https://www.iklimhaber.org/yeni-anket-kuresel-kamuoyu-zenginlere-yonelik-servet-vergisini-destekliyor/)).

## 4. Parties
- **Parties with verified wealth tax policies:**
  - **DEM Parti / HDP:** Adopted progressive wealth taxation in its *Demokratik Ekonomi Programı* ([DEM Parti](https://demparti.org.tr/tr/demokratik-ekonomi-programi/18366/)).
  - **Türkiye İşçi Partisi (TİP):** Formally committed to progressive wealth taxation in its *2. Kongre Çerçeve Belgesi* ([TİP](https://tip.org.tr/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2.-Kongre-Cerceve-Belgesi.pdf)).
  - **Emek Partisi (EMEP):** Active public campaign titled *"Patronlara ve zenginlere servet vergisi getirilsin!"* ([EMEP](https://emep.org/)).
  - **TÖP (Toplumsal Özgürlük Partisi):** Drafted and submitted Bill 2/2613 via MP Perihan Koca.
- **Parties without net wealth tax commitments:**
  - **AKP / MHP (Cumhur İttifakı):** The governing coalition has consistently rejected net wealth taxation; Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek reaffirmed in June 2024 that a wealth tax is not on the government's agenda.
  - **CHP (Cumhuriyet Halk Partisi):** While prominent individual economists and former MPs (such as Prof. Dr. Oğuz Oyan) advocate for wealth taxation, CHP's official platform focuses on structural tax justice, broadening income tax brackets, and lowering indirect taxes (ÖTV/KDV) rather than a general annual net wealth tax.

## 5. Government below national level
- **Structure:** 81 provinces (*iller*, ISO 3166-2:TR) and 973 districts (*ilçeler*). 30 provinces are organized as Metropolitan Municipalities (*Büyükşehir Belediyeleri*).
- **Taxation powers:** Under Article 73 of the Constitution of the Republic of Turkey, the authority to levy, alter, or abolish taxes is strictly reserved to the TBMM by statute (*"Vergi, resim, harç ve benzeri mali yükümlülükler kanunla konulur, değiştirilir veya kaldırılır"*). Municipalities have no statutory power to levy broad wealth taxes; municipal revenue is restricted to local property taxes (*emlak vergisi*, Law No. 1319), service fees, and shares of central tax collections distributed by İller Bankası.
- **Official units:** UN OCHA COD-AB-TUR dataset via Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX), matched with Turkish Ministry of Interior / TÜİK administrative code registry.
- **Local decision:** Sampled 10 metropolitan and district municipalities across different political leadership (Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir, Mersin, Diyarbakır, Kadıköy, Çankaya, Nilüfer, Seyhan, Sur). Yield: **0 qualifying municipal motions or resolutions**. Because local authorities lack any constitutional taxation competence and all policy advocacy targets national legislation, local council sweep is skipped (**aggregators only / skip local sweep**).

## 6. Search terms

  - `artan oranlı servet vergisi` (progressive wealth tax)
  - `net servet vergisi` (net wealth tax)
  - `zenginlere servet vergisi` (wealth tax on the rich)
  - `büyük servetlerin vergilendirilmesi` (taxation of large fortunes)
  - `süper zenginler vergisi` (super-rich tax)
  - `Servet Vergisi Kanun Teklifi` (wealth tax bill)
- **English primary terms:**
  - `Turkey wealth tax`, `net wealth tax Turkey`, `billionaire tax Turkey`, `TBMM wealth tax bill`.
- **Ambiguous and excluded terms:**
  - `Varlık Vergisi` (1942 WWII capital levy; exclude unless discussing modern revival or policy lineage).
  - `Değerli Konut Vergisi` (Law No. 7194, high-value residential housing tax; excluded as a narrow real-estate tax).
  - `Emlak Vergisi` (local immovable property tax; excluded).
  - `Veraset ve İntikal Vergisi` (inheritance and transfer tax; excluded unless combined with annual wealth tax).
