# Tanzania: area profile

Initial research only; the seed pass is incomplete. Search in English and Swahili.

- In 2026 Friedrich-Ebert-Stiftung Tanzania described Balozi Morwa’s publication as recommending a wealth tax: https://tanzania.fes.de/news/wealth-tax-launch.html
- The mainland government directory lists regional and local authorities, but the page does not supply a complete coded register or Zanzibar coverage: https://www.tanzania.go.tz/directories/local-government
- The Tanzanian Economic Review paper and UNCTAD report have been recovered and their recommendations recorded; see the evidence and leads files.

Parliament, party programmes, unions, organisations, regional and local records, suggestions, and map licensing remain to be investigated. No absence of support should be inferred from these gaps.

- Morwa’s December 2025 paper recommends broad-based wealth taxation and discusses net wealth taxation and exemptions: https://collections.fes.de/publikationen/download/pdf/1971253
- The 2018 OCHA administrative gazetteer contains 170 ADM2 districts, but it does not map one-to-one to today’s local councils (for example, Kondoa district and town councils). Its CC BY-IGO licence was checked in the HDX catalogue: https://data.humdata.org/dataset/cod-ab-tza
- Policy Forum describes debate on a net wealth tax; that web summary alone does not establish its endorsement: https://www.policyforum-tz.org/sw/node/6097

- The Controller and Auditor General’s FY 2024/25 report lists 184 mainland local authorities in appendix 5; names need regional and code matching: https://nao.go.tz/uploads/Annual_General_Report_for_Audit_of_PO-RALG__Regional_Administrations_and_Local_Government_Authorities_FY_2024-25.pdf
- Zanzibar’s 2023/24 government finance statistics list 12 local government authorities: https://www.ocgs.go.tz/publication-view/749
- The 2026 Policy Forum scanned brief proposes stakeholder consultation on a net wealth tax or a higher income tax rate, rather than clearly endorsing the net wealth tax. Policy Forum has a separate tentative C entry based on its signatures on tax-convention submissions: https://www.policyforum-tz.org/sw/node/6097
- The site suggestions queue returned no suggestions when checked on 2026-10-28.

- The NBS 2022 census ward shapefile contains names and region, district and council code components for 31 regions and 195 distinct council areas: 184 mainland and 11 Zanzibar. These census areas are now loaded provisionally. The Zanzibar government finance list has 12 authorities, including Zanzibar City Council, potentially an overlapping body: check its geographic relationship before adding it. The council code field is not globally unique on its own; no full code is asserted without confirmation. https://microdata.nbs.go.tz/index.php/catalog/49
- NBS publishes the shape data for public access but its metadata states copyright without an open licence, so the map is not used. https://www.nbs.go.tz/statistics/topic/gis

- The full UNCTAD report was recovered through an accessible text service and recommends a small wealth tax for Tanzania. Author Moritz Meier-Ewert is based outside Tanzania and is retained as a lead: https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/aldc2024d4_en.pdf
- geoBoundaries gbOpen ADM2 is CC BY 3.0 IGO but contains 170 districts, not the census council tier: https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/TZA/ADM2/

- A 2022 Swahili regional inequality report includes a foreword signed by Tanzanian bishop Nelson Kisare and discusses wealth taxation. The foreword does not itself endorse a wealth tax; a separate later statement is recorded at C, as described below: https://www.policyforum-tz.org/sites/default/files/2022-06/Ukosefu%20wa%20Usawa.pdf
- Broad searches of parliamentary and local terms did not identify a qualifying council or parliamentary position. They are not a systematic sweep of records.

- Oxfam, Development Finance International and Norwegian Church Aid published a 2022 SADC report, written by Matthew Martin, which recommends that governments introduce a tax on stocks of wealth including offshore wealth. Tanzania is included in the report’s regional scope. The three publishing organisations are international actors; their author is based outside Tanzania and is retained as a lead: https://www.policyforum-tz.org/sites/default/files/2022-06/Ukosefu%20wa%20Usawa.pdf

- In December 2024, Bishop Nelson Kisare proposed a progressive tax system based on individual wealth. His statement is graded C because the discussion also mentions property values, leaving its base unclear: https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/magazines/un-tax-convention-a-push-for-tax-justice-global-equality-4846894

- Netho Ndilito and Balozi Morwa co-authored a 2024 paper recommending broad-based wealth taxation and discussing a net-wealth tax and exemptions: https://quarterlyreview.net/ojs/index.php/aqssr/article/download/28/19/76

- Perplexity’s suggested Dodoma workshop participants, FES staff and labour organisations have been queued individually as leads. The event report establishes participation, not each person’s endorsement: https://tanzania.fes.de/news/wealth-tax-launch.html
- TJNA and Tax Justice Network’s 2024 climate-tax briefing draws on Tanzania collaboration with Policy Forum, but its published summary discusses tax justice and evasion, not a clear call for an actual wealth-stock tax: https://taxjusticeafrica.net/resources/news/new-briefing-paper-calls-reform-tax-systems-sustainably-finance-climate-action

- The 2023 Global Days of Action concept note names Public Services International and Tax Justice Network Africa among supporting organisations and calls for wealth taxes including taxation of unrealised gains. PSI later co-organised the 2026 Dodoma publication launch; TJNA collaborated with Tanzania’s Policy Forum on tax justice. Their entries are international campaign supporters, not proof of individual Tanzanian union support: https://globaltaxjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Concept-Note-English.pdf

- ActionAid International and Tax Justice Network also appear as supporters of the 2023 Global Days of Action campaign. ActionAid’s Arusha meeting discussed wealth taxation, and TJN has researched tax sovereignty in Tanzania. These entries are for the international organisations; the Tanzanian affiliate’s own wealth-tax position remains unverified. https://globaltaxjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Concept-Note-English.pdf

- The 2022 Tanzania CRI country profile was written by Matthew Martin and credits assistance from Gwamaka Mwakyanjala and Edmund Matotay. It proposes exploring a wealth tax; this is a research lead rather than evidence that either contributor endorsed it: https://www.policyforum-tz.org/sites/default/files/2022-06/leaving%20no%20one%20behind.pdf

- The 2025 and 2026 UN tax-convention submissions include Policy Forum and other Tanzania-based signatories. Their entries are graded C because the submissions mention existing wealth stocks but do not clearly define the proposed minimum tax base: https://gcap.global/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/English-Submission-WS1-UN-Tax-Convention-1.pdf

- A March 2024 Dar es Salaam workshop included a session on net wealth taxation. The published programme names international and other-country speakers but does not establish any Tanzanian participant’s endorsement: https://www.addistaxinitiative.net/event/closing-tax-gap-role-wealth-taxation
