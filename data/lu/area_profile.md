# Area profile — Luxembourg

## Scope
Seed pass, national level (Chamber, parties, unions). 12 cantons and 100 communes are registered from the official list, none searched. Languages: French, German and English. Date: 4 November 2026.

## Debate and sources
Luxembourg abolished the personal impôt sur la fortune with effect from 2006; today the tax is owed only by companies ([Guichet](https://guichet.public.lu/fr/entreprises/fiscalite/impots-benefices/benefices-patrimoine/impots-divers/impot-fortune.html)). That corporate levy is out of the definition. In the 2023 campaign déi Lénk (about 0.5% in 2018), déi gréng (new tax, EUR 1m allowance) and the KPL (progressive tax on the super-rich) called for a personal wealth tax; the LSAP promised only to analyse it, and the DP and CSV opposed it ([Tageblatt comparison by Berthereau and Scuto, 23 Sept 2023](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/57950/1/20230923_T_Berthereau_Scuto_Inegalites.pdf); [Paperjam/Delano](https://en.paperjam.lu/article/how-the-parties-feel-about-tax)). On 13 November 2024 the Chamber rejected, 40 to 20, Sam Tanson's motion for a global minimum tax on billionaires' wealth ([motion 4406](https://www.chd.lu/de/motion_resolution/4406)).

## R002 (4 November 2026)
The Chamber's own letter of 5 November 2024 (a text PDF, so checkable) shows Sam Tanson, as president of the déi gréng group, requesting a topical debate on the billionaire tax: A for her and for déi gréng. Tilly Metz, then a Green MEP, supports the Greens/EFA EU wealth tax with rates in a Paperjam interview of 18 December 2023. The nominal vote on motion 4406, OGBL/LCGB, the CSL, STATEC and University of Luxembourg economists and a Luxembourg poll were searched without a result; the CSL calls for taxing capital and wealth more but no explicit wealth tax was opened. Finance minister Gilles Roth said in October 2024 the government will not reintroduce it (reported by Les Frontaliers, which blocks scripts).
