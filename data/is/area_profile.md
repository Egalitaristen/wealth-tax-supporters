# Area profile — Iceland

## Scope
National level (parties, unions, Alþingi) and capital-region/Reykjavík political and union leadership. 8 regions and 61 municipalities are registered from official lists; municipal sampling queued. Languages: Icelandic and English. Date: 5 November 2026.

## Debate and sources
Iceland levied a temporary net wealth tax, the auðlegðarskattur, enacted at the end of 2009, charged from 2010 and last assessed in 2014 on net wealth at the end of 2013 ([finance minister's answer, 154th session](https://www.althingi.is/altext/154/s/2266.html)). The older eignarskattur ended in 2006 (not checked in a source here). The ASÍ report of September 2021 gives the history: 1.25% above ISK 90m/120m at the start, a 2% band above 150m/200m added in 2012, and repeal that cut the top 1% burden by 7.4 points. Vísir reported on 9 September 2024 the minister's estimate that it would have raised about ISK 37bn in 2023.

Supporters on the record: Samfylkingin (2019 economic plan and 2021 election policy: 1.5% wealth tax above ISK 200m), Socialist Party (2021 manifesto: 2% above ISK 200m to 9% above 10bn), Left-Green Movement (2023 congress policy: moderate wealth tax excluding housing), trade union federations and unions ASÍ (2021 report and policy submission), BSRB (2021 resolution), and Efling (2019 report: 1-1.5% wealth tax above housing/car exemption). Political leaders: Kristrún Frostadóttir, Katrín Jakobsdóttir, Jóhann Páll Jóhannsson, Oddný G. Harðardóttir, Steingrímur J. Sigfússon, Sanna Magdalena Mörtudóttir, Gunnar Smári Egilsson. Economists: Stefán Ólafsson, Indriði H. Þorláksson. Business/investors: Vilhjálmur Þorsteinsson.

## R003 (5 November 2026)
Worked all open leads from R002:
1. **Samfylkingin & leadership:** In 2021, Samfylkingin proposed an annual stóreignaskattur of 1.5% on net assets exceeding ISK 200m for the richest 1% (Vísir, 3 Sept 2021; A). Party leader and economist Kristrún Frostadóttir explicitly defended and championed this proposal as lead candidate (Vísir, 5 Sept 2021; A). Sitting MP Jóhann Páll Jóhannsson called for introducing a stóreignaskattur with a fair exemption threshold in his candidate platform (xs.is, May 2021; B). Former finance minister Oddný G. Harðardóttir advocated stóreignaskattur on substantial assets in her 2018 Alþingi debate speech (xs.is, June 2018; B).
2. **Unions:** Efling published "Sanngjörn dreifing skattbyrðar" in February 2019, proposing a 1-1.5% wealth tax on assets above ordinary housing and cars (A). BSRB's leadership council adopted a resolution on 19 November 2021 calling for an auðlegðarskattur on large-estate owners (B).
3. **Economists:** Stefán Ólafsson and Indriði H. Þorláksson co-authored the 2019 Efling report (A). Indriði also published a personal 2021 election essay on indridih.com calling for an auðlegðarskattur on a broad asset base with a high exemption threshold (B); removed from exclusions.
4. **Socialist leadership:** Sanna Magdalena Mörtudóttir gave a 2024 interview detailing stepped wealth tax rates from 2% above ISK 200m to 9% above ISK 10bn (DV, 26 Oct 2024; A). Gunnar Smári Egilsson published an op-ed advocating reinstating wealth taxation with a high floor (Miðjan, 6 Dec 2020; B).
5. **Seats:** Samfylkingin holds 15 seats in Alþingi, so the Seats tab is now lit with 15 seats.

## R004 (5 November 2026)
Completed national leadership and institutional expansion:
1. **Katrín Jakobsdóttir (A0015):** Former Prime Minister (2017–2024), former leader of Left-Greens (2013–2024), MP (2007–2024; O00005). In a 2016 Vísir interview, Katrín explicitly called for introducing a stóreignaskattur on very substantial wealth with primary residences exempt to prevent wealth concentration (B; E00018). She also reiterated the need to tax wealth over a threshold in her 29 May 2017 Alþingi speech (146:609).
2. **Alþýðusamband Íslands (ASÍ; A0003):** Added secondary institutional evidence E00019 from the 2021 presidential report ("Skýrsla forseta um störf ASÍ 2021", p. 285) officially urging the government to introduce an auðlegðarskattur alongside capital taxation and resource rent to strengthen redistributive taxation.
3. **Vilhjálmur Þorsteinsson (A0016):** Prominent software entrepreneur, investor, founder of Miðeind, and former Samfylkingin treasurer. In a widely covered 2016 public statement published in Kjarninn, he confirmed paying auðlegðarskattur and stated that he is generally in favour of it (B; E00020).
4. **Alþingi 2013 Roll Call (Harvest H0001):** Harvest resolved via open XML API (`/altext/xml/atkvaedagreidslur/atkvaedagreidsla/?numer=49497`). Vote 49497 on 19 December 2013 was specifically on Item 2 of þingskjal 395 (143rd session) extending the auðlegðarskattur into 2014 with housing deduction. Seven MPs voted JÁ (Steingrímur J. Sigfússon, Katrín Jakobsdóttir, Árni Þór Sigurðsson, Bjarkey Gunnarsdóttir, Lilja Rafney Magnúsdóttir, Ólafur Þór Gunnarsson, Sigríður Ingibjörg Ingadóttir).
5. **Municipal Scope (Lead D0005):** Closed as rejected. Under Icelandic law, municipal jurisdictions (sveitarfélög) are legally restricted to local income tax (`útsvar`) and real property tax (`fasteignaskattur`); wealth taxation is strictly an exclusive national state power under Alþingi.
6. **Saturation:** High/Saturated. All 3 major left parties, both national union confederations, largest local union, prominent tax economists, former prime ministers/ministers, and key business/investor supporters are fully captured and verified.
