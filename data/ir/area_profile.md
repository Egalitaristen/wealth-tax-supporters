## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Direct Taxes Reform Bill (2012–2013 / 1391–1392):** The 9th Majlis Economic Commission, chaired by Arsalan Fathi-Pour, drafted an explicit net wealth tax proposal (*māliyāt bar majmū'-e dārāyī-hā-ye manqūl va gheyr-e manqūl*) at a rate of 2 per thousand (0.2%) on total assets exceeding 2 billion tomans (~$650,000 USD at 2013 official rates), with an exemption for productive investments in manufacturing, agriculture, and mining.
- **Single-Asset Luxury Taxes (2020–present):** Annual Budget Laws (notably Band 'Ayn' Note 6) introduced annual taxes on luxury residences (*khāneh-hā-ye lūks*) and luxury vehicles (*khodrohā-ye lūks*). Under project criteria (SKILL.md Section 2), these single-asset excise/luxury taxes are excluded unless integrated into an aggregate net wealth tax.
- **Capital Gains & Speculation Tax (CGT, 2020–2024):** Extensive parliamentary and governmental focus centered on the Capital Gains Tax Bill (*māliyāt bar 'āyedī-ye sarmāyeh*), targeting short-term speculation in housing, foreign currency, gold, and automobiles. Leading MPs (such as Mohammad Reza Pour-Ebrahimi and Hossein Samsami) focused on CGT and explicitly opposed taxing financial assets or bank deposits.
- **Academic & Policy Advocacy:** Prominent institutional and academic economists (Hossein Raghfar, Vahid Shaghaghi Shahri, Masoud Saadatmehr, Afshin Bazgir) have consistently called for comprehensive wealth taxation and exit taxes on capital flight as essential structural tools to curb inequality and finance public goods under Piketty's capital framework.
- **Ministry of Economy Wealth Tax Symposium (December 2022):** The Economic Affairs Research Institute of the Ministry of Economic Affairs and Finance held a dedicated symposium on *Wealth Tax: Principles and Functions* (*Māliyāt bar Servat: Mabānī va Kārkard-hā*). Institute head Dr. Mohammad Javad Tavakkoli and former Tax Administration Strategic Council member Dr. Mohammad Reza Yazdi-Zadeh directly advocated introducing wealth taxation to regulate unproductive assets and counter extreme wealth concentration.

## 4. Parties
- Political competition in Iran operates through shifting political factions, alliances, and electoral lists rather than disciplined permanent political parties in the Western sense:
  - **Principlists (Usulgarayan):** Conservative factions. Figures like Arsalan Fathi-Pour supported wealth tax measures in the 2012–2013 Direct Taxes bill, while others focus on production incentives and anti-speculation CGT.
  - **Reformists (Eslāh-Talabān):** Generally support broader social safety nets and redistributive tax policies.
  - **Worker House (Khāneh-ye Kārgar):** Labor affiliate led by Alireza Mahjoub; advocates for worker wage protection and anti-speculation taxes.

## 5. Government below national level
- **Structure:** 31 Provinces (*ostān*) subdivided into 429 Counties (*shahrestān*).
- **Taxing Powers:** Taxation is entirely centralized at the national level under the Iranian National Tax Administration (INTA / *Sāzmān-e Omūr-e Māliyātī-ye Keshvar*). Provinces and counties have no independent statutory power to enact wealth or income taxes; local actors and provincial representatives direct tax policy proposals to the national Majlis and Ministry of Economic Affairs and Finance.
- **Boundaries:** Loaded from UN OCHA Common Operational Dataset on Administrative Boundaries (COD-AB) for Iran (`irn_admin_boundaries.xlsx` and `irn_admin_boundaries.geojson.zip`, CC BY-IGO).

## 6. Search terms
- Primary terms:
  - `مالیات بر ثروت` (*māliyāt bar servat* — wealth tax)
  - `مالیات بر دارایی` (*māliyāt bar dārāyī* — asset/wealth tax)
  - `مالیات بر مجموع دارایی‌ها` (*māliyāt bar majmū'-e dārāyī-hā* — net aggregate asset tax)
  - `مالیات بر خروج سرمایه` (*māliyāt bar khorūj-e sarmāyeh* — capital exit tax)
- Ambiguous / excluded terms to watch:
  - `مالیات بر خانه‌های لوکس` (luxury house tax — excluded single-property excise)
  - `مالیات بر خودروهای لوکس` (luxury car tax — excluded vehicle excise)
  - `مالیات بر عایدی سرمایه` (CGT / anti-speculation tax — excluded income/gains tax)
  - `مالیات بر خانه‌های خالی` (vacant homes tax — excluded regulatory housing levy)
