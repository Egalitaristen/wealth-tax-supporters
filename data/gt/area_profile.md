# Area profile — Guatemala

Seed pass of 2026-11-13 (run R001). Thin result: three supporters. Guatemala's fiscal debate runs on the Impuesto de Solidaridad, income-tax regimes, the IUSI property tax and the 2026 repeal of the inheritance tax; explicit calls for a tax on net wealth are rare.

## 1. Scope
- Area: Guatemala; type: country. Depth: Seed, national level only. Languages searched: Spanish and English.
- Register: 22 departamentos and 340 municipios from [OCHA / HDX COD-AB Guatemala](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/cod-ab-gtm) (valid 2019-02-07, CC BY-IGO), P-codes `GT01…` and `GT0101…`.

## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **No tax on personal net wealth exists.** Guatemala has the Impuesto de Solidaridad (ISO, a minimum tax on company turnover or assets, creditable against income tax), the IUSI on real estate, and (until Decree 6-2026, per ICEFI and press coverage; not opened `[verify]`) an inheritance, legacy and gift tax. None is counted here.
- **November 2015: CICIG commissioner Iván Velásquez** proposed, at an ICEFI forum on 10 November, a "transitory and temporary tax on large patrimonies" to double funding for the justice sector, saying it would reach no more than 5% of the population. CACIF and the Cámara de Industria rejected it; deputies Leonel Lira and Carlos Fión were reported as willing to discuss it. It was not enacted.
- **May 2020: Semilla's "Ley de Contribución Extraordinaria ante la Crisis"** would have taxed "el 0.1% súper-rico" (income above Q5 million a year) at a higher income-tax rate. Income, not wealth: recorded as an exclusion.
- **March 2025: Oxfam's Central America report** "Más Recursos para más Derechos" proposes a progressive 2.5% to 5% tax on fortunes above US$5m, launched with a quote from Oxfam's Guatemala director.
- **Opposition:** CACIF, Cámara de Industria, Universidad Francisco Marroquín columnists.
