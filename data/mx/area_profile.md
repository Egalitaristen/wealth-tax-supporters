# Area profile — Mexico

## 1. Scope
- Area: Mexico; country; **Seed** pass, started 2026-10-25.
- National, state and municipal levels are in scope. The register has 32 federal entities and 2,478 municipalities/alcaldías; Spanish was searched. Indigenous-language searching remains a gap.

## 2. Wealth-tax proposals
- A live proposal by José Luis Sánchez González would levy a progressive tax on individuals' net wealth above MXN100m: [PT parliamentary page](https://www.ptdiputados.org.mx/iniciativa.php?id=406).
- Amalia García Medina's April 2026 proposal would create annual 2–6% taxation of net wealth above MXN100m, according to a contemporaneous [legal analysis](https://www.perezllorca.com/es-mx/actualidad/nota-juridica/propuesta-de-reforma-fiscal-impuesto-a-las-herencias-y-grandes-fortunas/). The primary Senate material was unavailable when checked.
- Oxfam México, Fundar, Movimiento Ciudadano and the Alianza contra la Desigualdad have each published qualifying support. Fundar recommends an annual 3.5% tax on net wealth above MXN10,746m in its [2025 report](https://fundar.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Recomendaciones_justicia_fiscal-2004-2026.pdf).

## 3. Records and geography
- The Chamber's [Gaceta Parlamentaria](https://gaceta.diputados.gob.mx/) is the principal source for initiatives; the Senate official profile and open-data roster were partly inaccessible during this run.
- INEGI's [Catálogo Único](https://www.inegi.org.mx/servicios/catalogounico.html) supplies the place register and December 2025 Marco Geoestadístico boundaries. Its [Términos de Libre Uso](https://www.inegi.org.mx/inegi/terminos.html) allow copying, publication, adaptation and commercial exploitation with attribution.
- Municipalities may administer property-related charges, but a federal wealth tax needs national action. The six-unit local sample (Mexico City, Guadalajara, Monterrey, Tijuana, Puebla and Ecatepec) found no qualifying body adoption; local work will use campaign and regional-legislature sources before any broad municipal sweep.

## 4. Search terms and cautions
- Spanish: `impuesto a la riqueza`, `impuesto a las grandes fortunas`, `impuesto al patrimonio`, `patrimonio neto`, `milmillonarios`.
- Do not treat inheritance, luxury-property, income, capital-gains or generic progressive-tax proposals as wealth-tax support without an included design.

## 5. Follow-up coverage and next work
- R001 added national politicians, parties, civil-society organisations, campaigners, an economist and an editorial. It ran 84 recorded search queries and opened 28 distinct sources; several Senate/SIL and party pages were blocked or errored.
- R002 verified the eight principal authors of Oxfam México’s May 2026 *Financiar el futuro* report. The report endorses a 3% minimum effective levy on the net wealth of centimillionaires, so each named principal author is recorded with author-attribution evidence. A wide sweep of union, business, faith, culture, media and university material found one cautious supportive column by Luis Miguel Martínez Anzures, but no further qualifying records in the other sampled layers.
- R003 widened the search across coalition archives, public actions, named authors and legislative repositories. It added Accionar and Por las 40 horas for their public demand to tax billionaires, Ana Vicencio for an explicit Alianza campaign recommendation, Máximo Jaramillo as co-author of a qualifying article, and Alexandra Haas for a specific 8% proposal on fortunes above US$1bn. The sampled union, business and faith layers still yielded no qualifying record.
- R004 added a Fundar executive director and two named academic/economic commentators with attributable supporting statements. The broad sweep remains limited by the project’s exclusion of wealth-adjacent proposals such as inheritance-only, property-only and income-only taxation.
- R005 shifted to document-led extraction. It added thirteen people through a 2022 Oxfam México report with a formal eight-person author list and an explicit recommendation to tax wealth, plus separately attributable statements from Fundar, UNAM, CIDE and a business representative. This brings the Mexico register to 48 supporters.
- R006 added three people: the two formally credited authors of Fundar/ACIJ's 2024 report, which calls for a tax on multimillionaire wealth, and legislator Alejandro Rojas Díaz Durán for his explicit 2023 proposal. The official 2026 Chamber bill was read in full for additional sponsors; it names José Luis Sánchez González alone.
- R007 added three supporters from high-yield primary materials: the PT parliamentary group for its 2020 proposal of a 4.5% solidarity tax on large fortunes, OLEP for its 2021 proposal of a 4% tax on large fortunes, and Ifigenia Martínez Hernández for a 2021 Senate speech calling for a wealth tax. The five named signers of the 2023 constitutional initiative were already present, so they were not duplicated.
- R008 added Nuestro Presupuesto, which a Mexican report directly names as joining the 2022 protest demanding a wealth tax in Mexico. The 2026 federal annual-net-wealth bill has one sponsor already recorded; a separate Mexico City initiative was excluded because its operative measure concerns inheritances, not wealth stock.
- The same R008 sweep also added principal Oxfam México authors Laura Cruz Mendiola and Ervin Félix López. The 2026 report explicitly calls for progressive taxes on large fortunes; its other five principal authors were already represented.
- Next: follow Fundar, Oxfam and Alianza publication bylines; search each major national party’s formal platform and the federal bill database. Regional and municipal work remains low-yield and should be sampled before expansion.
- Regional and municipal research: use the national campaigns and state legislatures as aggregators. Do not expand the municipal sweep unless a new sample yields supporters.

## Review corrections (PR review, 2026-10-26)

Seventeen people were removed after the quotes and sources were read: Ifigenia Martínez Hernández (died 5 October 2024); six interviewees whose quote is not support (Pérez Garrido, Villarreal Páez, Carbajal, Campos Vázquez, del Ángel Mobarak, de la Mora); six credits on Oxfam México's 2022 report, which mentions a wealth tax only once, as a future step; two on a Fundar/ACIJ report that could not be read; and two on Oxfam's 2026 "Oligarquía o democracia" report. They are in exclusions.csv. The eight authors of Oxfam's 2026 "Financiar el futuro" report (which calls for a 3% minimum tax on the net wealth of the very rich), the five signers of the 2023 constitutional initiative and the PT group's 2020 proposal are now grade B: an author credit or a name in a gazette list is not the person's own statement, and the gazette pages could not be opened to read the initiative.
