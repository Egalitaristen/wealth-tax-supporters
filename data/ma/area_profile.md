## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Domestic tax landscape:** Morocco has no statutory annual net wealth tax (*impôt sur la fortune* / ضريبة على الثروة). The fiscal system administered by the Direction Générale des Impôts (DGI) relies primarily on indirect consumption taxes (TVA), corporate tax (IS), and personal income tax (IR), where wage earners bear over 70% of income tax receipts.
- **National Tax Conferences (Assises Nationales de la Fiscalité):** The issue of progressive wealth taxation was raised during the 2013 and 2019 National Tax Conferences. While the 2019 conference recommendations emphasized fiscal fairness, the resulting Framework Law No. 69-19 on Tax Reform focused on gradual harmonization of corporate and income tax rates, omitting a general net wealth tax.
- **Party platforms and joint electoral programs:**
  - **FGD & PSU Alliance:** In their joint electoral and political platform (*البرنامج السياسي والانتخابي 2026 — 2031: تحالف اليسار*), the Fédération de la Gauche Démocratique (FGD) and the Parti Socialiste Unifié (PSU) commit in Chapter 8 to establishing a national property register (*السجل الوطني للممتلكات*) and a progressive solidarity tax on comprehensive net wealth (*ضريبة التضامن على الثروة الشاملة*) in year 3 with explicit brackets: 0.5% (10M–50M DH), 1% (50M–100M DH), 1.5% (100M–500M DH), 2% (>500M DH), a 10M DH primary residence exemption, and a 60% total income tax shield.
- **Recurrent parliamentary amendments across Finance Bills (PLF):** Left-wing and opposition parliamentary groups regularly table formal amendments to the annual Finance Bill proposing an impôt sur la fortune on high net worth individuals:
  - **CDT (Confédération Démocratique du Travail):** In the House of Councillors, the CDT group tabled formal amendments to PLF 2025 proposing a net wealth tax of 0.20% between 10M and 100M DH, and 0.25% above 100M DH on bank accounts, real estate, and financial securities [3].
  - **Fatima Tamni (FGD):** Tabled formal parliamentary amendments to PLF 2025 specifying progressive wealth tax rates (0.20%–0.30%) on net assets from 10M to >500M DH [1].
  - **PPS (Parti du Progrès et du Socialisme):** The parliamentary group introduced amendments to PLF 2025 proposing an annual 1% net wealth tax on total wealth exceeding 50M DH [1].
  - **Groupe Haraki (Mouvement Populaire):** In PLF 2026 debates, the Haraki group introduced an amendment proposing an "Annual Net Wealth Tax" (*الضريبة السنوية على صافي الثروة*) on resident individuals with net wealth exceeding 50M DH, at progressive rates from 0.25% to 1% [4].
  - **Omar Balafrej (former FGD MP):** Long-standing proponent of progressive taxation of net wealth and inheritance in parliament and media [2].
  - **Nabila Mounib (PSU MP):** Regularly challenges government budgets for failing to broaden the tax base and lacking a progressive tax on wealth [5].
- **Academic and expert voices:**
  - **Najib Akesbi:** Leading Moroccan public finance economist and university professor (IAV Hassan II), who consistently advocates for a net wealth tax (*impôt sur la fortune*) to end fiscal taboos, address growing inequality, and compel capital into productive investments rather than speculation [8].
- **Civil society and international advocacy:**
  - **Oxfam au Maroc:** Landmark report *Un Maroc égalitaire, une taxation juste* (2019) recommending progressive taxation of net wealth stock to reduce structural inequalities [6]. Country Director Xavier Duvauchelle advocated publicly in national media (*L'Opinion*) for the redistributive necessity of an ISF [7].
  - **Civil Society Joint Memorandum (AMDH, Transparency Maroc, Espace Associatif):** In their joint memorandum on the 2023 Finance Bill (PLF 2023), the Moroccan Association of Human Rights (AMDH), Transparency Maroc, and Espace Associatif formally demanded establishing a tax system on wealth and unproductive assets (*إرساء نظام جبائي على الثروة والممتلكات غير المنتجة*) [9].

## 4. Legislatures and where their records are
- **Chambre des Représentants (House of Representatives):** Lower house, 395 members (`https://www.chambredesrepresentants.ma`). Budget amendments, committee sessions, and plenary debates are published on the website and reported in national press (*Al3omk*, *Hespress*, *Le Matin*).
- **Chambre des Conseillers (House of Councillors):** Upper house, 120 members representing territorial communities, professional chambers, and trade unions (`https://www.chambredesconseillers.ma`). Union groups (including CDT) have direct parliamentary representation and submit budget amendments here.

## 5. Parties
- **Fédération de la Gauche Démocratique (FGD):** Left-wing party alliance; formally committed in platform to a progressive net wealth tax.
- **Parti Socialiste Unifié (PSU):** Democratic socialist party; co-authored joint platform for progressive net wealth tax.
- **Parti du Progrès et du Socialisme (PPS):** Opposition socialist party, 22 seats; formally sponsors wealth tax amendments in PLF.
- **Mouvement Populaire (MP):** Opposition party, 28 seats; Groupe Haraki sponsored annual net wealth tax amendments in PLF 2026.
- **Union Socialiste des Forces Populaires (USFP):** Socialist party; proposed an amendment in PLF 2026 strictly targeting real estate assets over 5M DH (excluded under Section 2 as property tax).

## 6. Government below national level
- **Structure:** 12 administrative regions (Régions) established under the 2015 advanced regionalisation, subdivided into 75 provinces and prefectures (Provinces et Préfectures).
- **Taxation powers:** Subnational regional and provincial councils receive transfers from national taxes (IR, IS, TVA) and local fees; they have no power to create or levy a net wealth tax.
- **Map:** Built from geoBoundaries gbOpen (`geoBoundaries-MAR-ADM2` and `ADM1`), licensed under ODbL 1.0 (`data/ma/boundaries.json`). Contains 75 local units and 12 region outlines.

## 7. Search terms
- Arabic: `ضريبة على الثروة` (dariba 'ala al-tharwa), `ضريبة الثروة` (daribat al-tharwa), `الضريبة السنوية على صافي الثروة` (annual net wealth tax), `ضريبة على كبار الأثرياء` (tax on the super-wealthy), `تضريب الثروات`.
- French: `impôt sur la fortune`, `ISF`, `impôt progressif sur le patrimoine`, `taxe sur les grandes fortunes`, `justice fiscale`.
- Ambiguous phrases:
  - *الثروة العقارية* / *fortune immobilière*: Target property or real estate alone; excluded under Section 2.
  - *المظاهر الخارجية للثراء* / *signes extérieurs de richesse*: Taxes on luxury goods (cars, yachts); excluded under Section 2.
  - *زكاة* (*zakat*): Religious charity occasionally discussed alongside taxation; excluded unless formally proposed as statutory tax legislation.
