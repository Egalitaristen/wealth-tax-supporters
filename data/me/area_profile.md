# Area profile — Montenegro

## 1. Scope
First pass (2026-11-04), national level, in Montenegrin/Serbian and English. Very little was found; this is a coverage note, not evidence of no support.

## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
No wealth tax exists. In 2012 the finance minister rejected union, NGO and party calls for progressive taxation (income). The 2022 "Europe Now" reform and the 2023-24 extra-profit levy are not wealth taxes. A 2018 report said initiatives for a luxury tax went unanswered.

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
Skupština (skupstina.me, zakoni.skupstina.me). The bills registry was not searched directly.

## 4. Parties
No party found with a wealth-tax position. Programmes of Levica, URA, Evropa sad, Demokrate, DPS, SDP not read.

## Round 2 (2026-11-04)
Montenegro still has one grade-C row (Kostić, 2012), and RFE shows he listed yachts, houses, boats, meadows and vineyards, so it may mean property taxation. Searches for Levica, Evropa sad, URA, Demokrate, SDP, unions and think tanks found nothing; the only Skupština bill seen is a Demokratska Crna Gora bill taxing officials' state housing benefits at 100% (not a wealth tax). The Skupština registry itself is still unsearched.

## Round 3 (2026-11-06)
Alejna Pepić publicly discussed her master’s thesis in [Biznis CG](https://bizniscg.me/pepic-oporezivanjem-najbogatijih-do-jednakosti-u-crnogorskom-drustvu): it recommends a gradual, carefully designed potential wealth tax focused on the richest, subject to institutional capacity. The original thesis and exact survey question were not obtained; the online convenience sample is not recorded as a representative poll. An indexed [RTCG report](https://rtcg.me/vijesti/ekonomija/200947/nema-dodatnog-poreza-za-64-milionera.html) repeats Kostić’s 2018 remarks on taxing investment in personal wealth, but does not clearly establish an annual stock tax. Parliamentary registry and party programmes remain open; no local sweep was conducted. Earlier run plans mentioning FBiH concern Bosnia and Herzegovina, not Montenegro.

## Round 4 (2026-11-06)
The [university research register](https://www.fzp.ac.me/akademski_radovi_pdf.php?godina=2026) confirms Pepić’s thesis and its supervisor but has no link to the text. The [2013 parliamentary transcript](https://zakoni.skupstina.me/zakoni/web/dokumenta/sjednice-skupstine/45/555-.pdf) shows Srđan Milić discussing undeclared wealth and a special tax on the rich, but does not clearly establish a qualifying wealth-stock tax. [RFE’s interview](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/maric-buducnost-crnorogskog-sdpa-ce-bitno-zavisiti-od-izbora/24891134.html) attributes a wealth-tax alternative to unnamed critics, not the interviewee. The [Democrats’ programme](https://demokrate.me/uploads/Program_DCG_02f4e1fe48.pdf) had no exact “porez na bogatstvo” match; this is not an exhaustive review. Indexed SSCG and Nikolaidis/Pajović articles suggest promising leads but could not be opened, so neither is added as evidence. The parliamentary portal was opened, not exhaustively searched.

## Round 5 (2026-11-06)
Opened the [SSCG statement](https://www.portalanalitika.me/clanak/88552--sscg-pozitivan-iskorak-skupstine-da-se-ne-oporezuju-zarade-do-479-eura), which calls in 2013 for taxation of enormous wealth alongside other measures, and the [2024 Nikolaidis–Pajović article](https://www.aktuelno.me/clanak/nikolaidis-i-pajovic-evropa-sad-nudi-platu-od-1000-eura-mi-predlazemo-program-od-1000-eura-svima-cak-i-ako-ne-radite), which calls for taxing substantial accumulated assets and lists multiple asset classes. Both are tentative because their designs are unspecified.

## Round 6 (2026-11-06)
The [RTCG 2018 report](https://rtcg.me/vijesti/ekonomija/200947/nema-dodatnog-poreza-za-64-milionera.html) quotes Kostić advocating taxation of investments that build personal wealth; it does not clearly propose taxing the stock. The separate 2012 evidence remains tentative. [Vesko Pejak’s 2019 remarks](https://old.dan.co.me/?clanak=722691&datum=2019-11-24&nivo=3&rubrika=Drustvo) are labelled wealth taxation by the reporter but his concrete proposal concerns additional homes, outside scope. A [collection of 2009 election programmes](https://media.cgo-cce.org/2013/09/cgo-izdavastvo-parlamentarni-izbori-2009-doprinos-razvoju-politickog-pamcenja-u-crnoj-gori.pdf) contains a proposal for wealth and cumulative property taxation; its 2009 date is outside the evidence window despite later republication. Search for a later position before attributing support.

## Round 7 (2026-11-06): civil society and geographic sample
A [2012 Vijesti report](https://www.vijesti.me/vijesti/drustvo/300258/marovici-dukanovici-i-nikcevici-bogati-imaju-sve-vise-siromasni-tonu) describes union representatives Sandra Obradović and Ljiljana Krivokapić favouring a possible wealth tax; it does not specify its design. Sociologist Risto Kilibarda, also quoted, died in 2013 and is excluded. Searches sampled Podgorica, Nikšić, Bar, Budva, Cetinje, Pljevlja, Bijelo Polje, Herceg Novi and Ulcinj, plus national unions, MANS, CGO and Institut alternativa; these were indexed web searches, not systematic searches of local records. No additional local supporter was verified. Albanian-language search was also attempted. A proposed [tax on unexplained assets](https://rtcg.me/vijesti/drustvo/756524/bojicev-zakon-ko-ima-mora-znati-odakle-mu-je.html) targets assets without proven lawful origin, not a general wealth tax. Further search should prioritise original union resolutions and civil-society archives before expanding the local sweep.
