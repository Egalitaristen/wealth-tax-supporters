# Area profile — Romania

## Scope and coverage

Initial national research, 31 October 2026. National, county and municipal levels are in scope, but the national pass remains incomplete. Romanian and English web searches were used. No local authority has been sampled. The register is not a measure of opposition or exhaustive coverage.

## Evidence

Cornel Ban and Cristian Pop authored the May 2026 study *Cum reconstruim bugetul?*, proposing a 0.5% annual tax on net financial wealth in its social-democratic scenario. Its heading and examples use a €1 million threshold while another passage gives €2 million; the evidence records this inconsistency. In a separate June 2026 interview, Ban specifies 0.5% annually above €2 million in net financial wealth. The publisher expressly says the authors' views do not necessarily reflect its own, so it is not entered as a supporter.

The Senate's [L299/2025 register](https://www.senat.ro/Legis/Lista.aspx?cod=26923) names six initiators of the National Solidarity Law, registered on 25 June 2025. Its [draft](https://www.senat.ro/legis/PDF/2025/25L299FG.PDF?nocache=true) proposes an annual levy on total wealth with tiers starting at €10 million; its threshold unusually combines income, gains, movable assets and buildings, and its treatment of debt is unclear. It was forwarded to the Chamber; a committee issued a rejection report in March 2026. Dan Chitic published his draft and identified himself as its author. In a [2023 statement](https://www.danchitic.ro/scrieri/2023.html), he attributed the initiative to Coaliția pentru Națiune, which is also recorded as a supporter. Sponsorship establishes support for the specific proposal, not necessarily for other wealth taxes. Party labels on these records reflect their unaffiliated status at introduction and need refresh; no current seats are asserted.

Alexandra Rusu co-authored a 2019 fiscal study with Ban, but the study credits her contribution to its case study. She is excluded pending evidence tying her to its wealth-tax recommendation.

The 2010 [L361 bill](https://www.senat.ro/Legis/Lista.aspx?cod=14973) proposed 0.5% on net wealth over €500,000 and was rejected. Its initiator Mugurel Surupăceanu, union leader Bogdan Hossu and CNSLR-Frăția general secretary Sorin Stan supported the proposal; the two union representatives asked for changes. Cartel Alfa, CNSLR-Frăția, CSN Meridian and Blocul Național Sindical are recorded for the reported support of their representatives; Meridian representative Ion Popescu is also named. The unions sought changes to the proposal.

In December 2023, MEP Maria Grapini welcomed a citizens’ initiative to tax large fortunes. Researcher Nicoleta Mihăilă called in 2024 for progressive taxes on citizens’ assets; senator Victoria Stoiciu and radio commentator Virgil Natea made their own calls in 2020 and 2023 respectively. These are individual statements, not positions attributed to their employers or parties.

SENS called in a [June 2025 party statement](https://agerpres.ro/comunicate/2025/06/17/comunicat-de-presa---partidul-sens--1460156) for a 1% wealth tax above 100 annual average gross salaries. Its [political programme](https://ro.scribd.com/document/1057435445/Httpscdn-prod-Website-Files-com66c3ce72df13d92462a05286672f6bdc8a0104824fcfcf03-Program20Politic20SENS202025-202820V1-1-2) specifies net wealth including financial assets. Grapini also [directly endorsed the European initiative in July 2023](https://pucl.ro/europarlamentarul-pusl-maria-grapini-sustine-initiativa-cetateneasca-europeana-privind-impozitarea-marilor-averi/), independently of her separate domestic proposal concerning unjustified wealth. MEP Nicolae Ștefănuță co-signed a [2023 European Parliament amendment](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/A-9-2023-0336-AM-001-004_EN.pdf) calling for levies on high wealth and international discussions toward a progressive wealth tax; it does not specify the base or rate. The amendment was rejected 232–331, with 49 abstentions. The [individual roll call](https://howtheyvote.eu/votes/162327), filtered to Romania and votes for, identifies nine supporters, including seven added in the next run; this is the vote on the amendment itself, not the final vote on the broader report. Party labels are not interpreted as a party-wide endorsement. In a separate [2 April 2025 roll call](https://howtheyvote.eu/votes/174622), Gheorghe Piperea and Nicolae Ștefănuță voted for an amendment proposing a 3% minimum tax on wealth over €100 million; it was rejected 145–529, with ten abstentions. Other MEPs’ votes on different proposals do not automatically retract their 2023 positions.

On 27 February 2024, Maria Grapini and Nicolae Ștefănuță also voted for [amendment 10](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/A-9-2024-0053-AM-005-013_SK.pdf), which called for a European wealth tax as an EU own resource without specifying the base or rate. The [official roll call](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bf/OJ_C_202501589_of_2025_-_FR_French.pdf) records the amendment as rejected by 140–463, with 21 abstentions.

Cumpăna mayor Mariana Gâju was [reported by the Constanța branch of the Romanian communes association](https://acor-constanta.ro/2024/02/01/cea-de-a-159-a-sesiune-plenara-a-comitetului-european-al-regiunilor/) to have supported the European citizens’ wealth-tax initiative in 2024. The report’s direct quotation discusses multinational profits; the attribution to the initiative is reported rather than directly quoted. Her inclusion does not mean that Cumpăna’s council has adopted a motion or that a local sweep was completed.

Economist and commentator Radu Golban advocated a [progressive annual net wealth tax](https://www.cotidianul.ro/impozitam-painea-nu-miliardele/) in June 2025, starting at €5 million and explicitly distinguishing it from a luxury tax that excludes financial portfolios.

## Leads and cautions

The 2024 prospective governing coalition programme called for progressive taxation of large fortunes but did not define the tax base; its later identified package targeted expensive houses and cars. Agerpres reported Sorin Grindeanu and PSD calling for higher taxation of large fortunes in July 2025. The contemporary [package description](https://www.profit.ro/taxe-si-consultanta/ultima-ora-ideile-psd-pachetul-2-masuri-bugetare-taxare-mare-marile-averi-criptomonede-castiguri-capital-cheltuieli-nedeductibile-multinationale-limitari-companiile-stat-insolvente-22107553) identifies higher taxes on expensive houses and cars. Those particular leads were excluded; different, qualifying statements could change that assessment. A 2010 UNPR proposal found in Mediafax covers buildings, aircraft and boats; it is not sufficient evidence of a tax on net wealth or a broad class of financial assets.

Deputy Constantin Dascălu supported a wealth tax in principle during the [2012 Chamber debate](https://www.cdep.ro/ords/pls/steno/steno2015.stenograma?idm=4&ids=7111) but rejected Surupăceanu’s specific bill and cited an alternative associated with UNPR, whose reported design covers property and luxury assets. His preferred tax base remains unclear; he remains a lead pending qualifying evidence. Economist Sebastian Buhai proposed progressive net asset taxation in 2025, but the account says financial assets are taxed when gains are realised, leaving the base and timing unclear; he remains a lead. Former president Ion Iliescu is excluded because he died in 2025.

The housing-rights coalition Blocul pentru Locuire called for taxing large fortunes in its [2020 manifesto](https://www.criticatac.ro/manifest-pentru-dreptate-locativa-impotriva-pandemiei-capitalismului-si-rasismului/), but its accompanying policy lists income, capital, profits and property, leaving the taxable stock unclear. The [Blocul Suveranist Român policy page](https://partidulbloculsuveranistroman.ro/misiunea-bsr/solidaritate-sociala/) describes a qualifying tax on total wealth but has no visible publication date; the [2023 alliance launch report](https://www.stiripesurse.ro/s-a-format-blocul-suveranist-roman-cine-sunt-cele-7-partide-care-au-decis-sa-concureze-aur-in-anul-electoral-2024_3152243.html) confirms that a same-name party was one of seven alliance members, but the report does not establish the alliance’s tax base. Both are leads, not yet supporters in the register.

## Geography and seats

The geography uses the INS SIRUTA S1 2026 register, [normalized and republished by romania/siruta](https://github.com/romania/siruta) under CC BY 4.0. It contains 42 county-level units and 3,187 local entries: 3,181 municipalities, towns and communes including Bucharest, plus its six sectors. Names are title-cased, hierarchy and official SIRUTA codes retained. All remain pending. Boundaries were not loaded. The [geo-spatial.org catalogue](https://geo-spatial.org/descarcare/date/administrative-boundaries/) offers SIRUTA-coded simplified polygons as well as lines. A [republication](https://github.com/National-Youth-Foundation/harta-romania-uat) describes them as CC BY-SA 4.0; verify the original licence and its compatibility with this repository before conversion. Broad searches of Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara, Iași, Brașov and Constanța yielded property-tax references but no qualifying council measure; these were exploratory web searches, not completed council-record sweeps. There is no seats chart. Six newer MEP records were checked against the European Parliament’s Romanian member list dated 8 May 2026; the broader chamber and other offices remain to be refreshed.
