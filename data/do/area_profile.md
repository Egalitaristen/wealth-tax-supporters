## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **No net-wealth tax exists.** The country taxes real estate through the Impuesto al Patrimonio Inmobiliario (IPI, 1% above an exempt threshold), a property tax and not counted here. A CEPAL study puts Dominican net-wealth-tax revenue at about 0.06% of GDP, the lowest it lists ([CEPAL](https://repositorio.cepal.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/44ae183d-d984-44bc-8965-adcf49ba3bcf/content), snippet only `[verify]`).
- **2024 and 2026 reforms** (Ley de Modernización Fiscal, withdrawn in October 2024; Ley 30-26, promulgated June 2026 `[verify]`) raise income taxes on top earners and large companies and adjust the IPI. None creates a wealth tax; the government's own pillar, "incrementar las recaudaciones provenientes del patrimonio de las personas", refers to property ([Acento](https://acento.com.do/el-financiero/la-propuesta-de-gobierno-a-la-reforma-fiscal-que-los-ricos-paguen-mas-8986917.html)). Recorded as an exclusion.
- **Civil society** (Ciudad Alternativa, Foro Ciudadano, Copadeba, FES, CIPAF) asks for a fiscal pact and names low taxation of wealth and property as a problem, but proposes inheritance, luxury-goods and dividend measures, not a wealth tax. Exclusions.
- **Oxfam** (economist Rosa Cañete) presented the January 2026 regional report in Santo Domingo and called for taxing wealth.
