## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Current status:** Vietnam has no statutory net wealth tax (*thuế tài sản ròng*) or billionaire minimum tax (*thuế tỷ phú*). The tax system is predominantly based on indirect taxes (VAT, Special Consumption Tax) and direct taxes on income and corporate profits.
- **The 2018 Draft Law on Property Tax (*Dự án Luật Thuế tài sản*):** In April 2018, the Ministry of Finance (*Bộ Tài chính*) published a draft property tax law proposing annual levies on housing exceeding 700 million or 1 billion VND (0.3%–0.4%), non-agricultural land, luxury cars valued over 1.5 billion VND, aircraft, and yachts. The draft triggered widespread national debate and severe public pushback over double taxation and low thresholds. It was shelved in 2018 and never introduced to the National Assembly plenary. (Excluded under section 2 as a gross physical property / single-asset tax).
- **Ongoing real estate and second-home tax debate:** The Communist Party of Vietnam's Central Committee adopted Resolution 18-NQ/TW in June 2022 on reforming land policy, directing higher tax rates on individuals holding multiple properties or leaving land vacant (*thuế bất động sản thứ hai, thuế đất bỏ hoang*). Economists and National Assembly deputies actively debate these real estate measures, but they remain single-asset property taxes rather than broad net wealth taxes.
- **International and Civil Society advocacy:** Oxfam in Vietnam (*Oxfam tại Việt Nam*) actively champions progressive wealth taxation on multi-millionaires and billionaires through national releases of global reports (*Survival of the Richest*, *Inequality Inc.*, *Takers Not Makers*) calling for up to 5% net wealth taxes (*thuế tài sản ròng*) to tackle extreme wealth concentration and fund social protection. Research into other civil society networks (VATJ, ActionAid Vietnam, CEPEW) confirms that their advocacy is directed toward corporate tax incentives, illicit financial flows, and reducing regressive VAT burdens rather than net wealth taxation.

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
- **National Assembly of Vietnam (*Quốc hội Việt Nam*):**
  - Unicameral legislature comprising 499 deputies (15th National Assembly, term 2021–2026).
  - Official portal: [quochoi.vn](https://quochoi.vn)
  - Parliamentary records and library: [thuvienso.quochoi.vn](https://thuvienso.quochoi.vn)
  - Press and session coverage: [daibieunhandan.vn](https://daibieunhandan.vn)
  - Roll calls: Plenary votes are typically electronic and summary-level; specific roll calls on wealth tax bills do not exist because no wealth tax bill has reached the plenary floor.
- **Sub-national bodies:** People's Councils (*Hội đồng nhân dân*) at provincial and district levels possess no constitutional or statutory authority to establish taxes under the State Budget Law (*Luật Ngân sách Nhà nước*).

## 4. Parties
- **Communist Party of Vietnam (*Đảng Cộng sản Việt Nam* - CPV):** The sole ruling party under Article 4 of the Constitution. Holds 485 of 499 seats in the 15th National Assembly. Party resolutions emphasize socialist-oriented market development, poverty reduction, and land management reforms; no policy on annual net wealth taxation.
- **Independents / Non-party deputies:** 14 deputies in the 15th National Assembly elected under the Vietnam Fatherland Front (*Mặt trận Tổ quốc Việt Nam*).

## 5. Government below national level
- **Structure:**
  - Region (63 units): 58 provinces (*tỉnh*) and 5 centrally-governed cities (*thành phố trực thuộc trung ương*: Hà Nội, TP. Hồ Chí Minh, Hải Phòng, Đà Nẵng, Cần Thơ). Official 2-digit GSO codes `01` to `96`.
  - Local (705 units): District-level administrative units (*quận*, *huyện*, *thị xã*, *thành phố thuộc tỉnh*). Official 3-digit GSO codes `001` to `973`.
- **Taxing power:** Direct and indirect taxation is exclusively a national legislative power reserved to the National Assembly. Sub-national councils cannot levy local wealth or income taxes.
- **Official source for unit codes:** General Statistics Office of Vietnam (*Tổng cục Thống kê* - GSO), Decision No. 124/2004/QĐ-TTg and updates; mirrored in the `hanhchinhvn` dataset.
- **Local decision:** Skip unit-by-unit sweep. Sub-national councils have no tax authority; local debate on wealth taxation is absent from district and provincial records.

## 6. Search terms
- **Included terms:**
  - `thuế tài sản ròng` (net wealth tax)
  - `thuế người giàu` / `đánh thuế người giàu` (taxing the rich)
  - `thuế người siêu giàu` / `thuế giới siêu giàu` (tax on the super-rich)
  - `thuế tỷ phú` / `thuế tỉ phú` (billionaire tax)
  - `thuế tài sản` (property/wealth tax; check context)
- **Excluded terms (single-asset taxes or separate tax categories):**
  - `thuế bất động sản` (real estate tax)
  - `thuế nhà đất` (housing and land tax)
  - `thuế sử dụng đất phi nông nghiệp` (non-agricultural land use tax)
  - `thuế thu nhập cá nhân` (personal income tax)
  - `thuế thừa kế` / `thuế chuyển nhượng bất động sản` (inheritance / property transfer tax)
  - `thuế tiêu thụ đặc biệt` (special consumption tax on luxury vehicles/yachts)

## 9. Known networks
- **Oxfam tại Việt Nam:** Active advocate of tax justice and wealth taxation to combat inequality.
- **Liên minh Công bằng Thuế Việt Nam (VATJ):** Coalition of research institutes and CSOs advocating tax transparency and corporate tax expenditure reforms.
- **Viện Nghiên cứu Kinh tế và Chính sách (VEPR):** Independent economic think tank under VNU University of Economics and Business; leading academic commentator on tax proposals.
