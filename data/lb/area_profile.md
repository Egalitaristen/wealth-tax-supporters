## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **No wealth tax exists.** Lebanon taxes income, inheritance and registered built property.
- **September 2017.** Walid Joumblatt tweets that a wealth tax "as in France" must be imposed (`A0002`).
- **November 2019.** After the financial collapse, economist Lydia Assouad lists an exceptional tax on private capital, in particular real estate, as an option (`A0004`).
- **2020.** Ghassan Dibeh's critique of the government's April 2020 recovery plan gives a 2% tax on net wealth over USD 1 million as an example (`A0005`). UN ESCWA's July 2020 paper models a solidarity levy of 0.9% to 3.6% of top-decile wealth to close the poverty gap (`A0009`).
- **January to March 2021.** Assouad's Carnegie paper calls for a one-time wealth tax on the richest, 10% on billionaires, and an annual wealth tax later (`A0004`).
- **14 April 2021.** MP Taymour Joumblatt (Progressive Socialist Party) tables a bill for a progressive wealth tax on wealth of USD 1 million or more: 0.5% up to 7% above USD 1 billion, with exemptions for owner-occupied homes and some other assets. It was not put on the plenary agenda (`A0001`; Legal Agenda).
- **May 2021.** The Arab Reform Initiative paper by Bifani, Daher, Assouad and Diwan asks for a one-off wealth tax to be considered (`A0006` to `A0008`).
- **2022.** The Lebanese Communist Party's election programme proposes a 2% tax on individuals' net wealth above USD 1 million (`A0003`).
- **2025 to 2026.** The debate moved to bank resolution and the financial gap law (cabinet draft December 2025), a Sayrafa windfall tax and a dispute between the Finance Ministry and the banks over tax on crisis-year profits. None is a tax on net wealth (`X0001` to `X0003`, `X0005`).
