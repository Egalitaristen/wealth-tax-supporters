# New Zealand — seed and broad follow-up, 25 October 2026

## 1. Scope
National supporters since 2010, with a sampled search for local support. R001 is the seed; R002 follows the leads and makes wider political, civil-society, economist, commentator, wealthy/business and faith sweeps. This is not an exhaustive register. Every territorial authority is mapped even where its records have not been searched. No individual is included merely for belonging to a party or coalition. The site suggestions queue was read again for R002: zero new suggestions.

## 2. Proposals and history
The [Greens' 21 June 2026 announcement](https://www.greens.org.nz/greens_commit_to_tax_the_super_rich_and_large_corporates_fairly_cut_income_tax_for_96) proposes 2.5% on net assets above NZ$10m, excluding family homes. This differs from its 2023 NZ$2m individual threshold. [Te Pāti Māori's 2026 manifesto](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=11dbbk3BTNigHUb4xANPnTKxb2TC9dAgg) proposes 1.5% above NZ$2m, 2% above NZ$5m and 2.5% above NZ$10m. Its [2023 policy](https://img.scoop.co.nz/media/pdfs/2307/Te_Pti_Mori_TAX_POLICY.pdf) used different rates. [Alliance's September 2026 policy](https://allianceparty.nz/alliance-tax-plan-first-20000-income-tax-free/) proposes an annual 2.5% above NZ$2m per individual.

## 3. Legislature
[Hansard](https://hansard.parliament.nz/) and party statements were searched for wealth-tax support and former ministers' positions. No qualifying roll-call list was harvested in this seed. Do not infer support from an income-tax vote. R001 left Robertson and Parker as leads because valedictory language about taxing assets allows several designs. R002 found direct interview evidence; see below. The Treasury's [2023 tax advice](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2023-07/b23-tax-4791453.pdf) remains useful design context.

## 4. Parties
Greens, Te Pāti Māori, Alliance and the current Progressive Party have qualifying policies. The [PHCC 2026 party survey](https://www.phcc.org.nz/briefing/election-2026-will-parties-tax-policies-support-public-health-and-equity) distinguishes Labour's capital-gains policy and Opportunity's land tax; these alone do not meet this database's definition. A politician's own statement is required separately from a party policy. List MPs can remain at the country root where a finer public base was not verified; James Shaw is a former list MP, not a missing constituency.

## R002 findings and decisions
Direct interviews establish [Grant Robertson's personal support](https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/06-04-2024/at-the-cricket-with-former-mp-grant-robertson) and [David Parker's position following the rejected tax proposal](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/politics/david-parker-says-holding-revenue-portfolio-was-untenable-after-wealth-and-capital-gains-taxes-scrapped/). Neither is evidence of Labour adopting that policy. The Treasury advice contains unmarked ministerial recommendation fields; its officials are not imported as supporters.

Candidate-provided priorities, direct debate reporting and authored policy advocacy supplied further Green politicians and campaigners. The [Win the Wealth Tax interview](https://systemchange.nz/2024/12/08/should-socialists-join-nz-labour/) establishes both the campaign and Sachs's stance. Te Raukura O’Connell Rapira published earlier articles as Laura O’Connell Rapira: the two names were merged, retaining both publications. The current [Progressive Party policy](https://www.progressiveparty.co.nz/policies-3/) is distinct from both the Greens and Jim Anderton's former party; its date refers to the current page revision year, not an assumed original adoption date.

The economist/commentator sweep added authored advocacy from Easton, Hickey, Eaqub, Marriott, Bradbury and Gordon Campbell. Eaqub's preference for a broad wealth tax is qualified by political feasibility, which is stated in his notes. St John and Baucher's concrete deemed-return housing proposal is outside the definition. No generic international Piketty/Zucman statements were imported as NZ evidence.

The civil-society sweep followed the Tax Working Group’s 2018 summary to all six named organisational net-wealth-tax supporters (H0001), including original NZCCSS and Christchurch East Labour submissions. Historical endorsements are dated as such; the local Labour branch does not establish national Labour policy. ActionStation, anti-poverty and equality networks supplied further own statements. The two ambiguous wealth/capital-tax statements remain grade C. EcuAction’s five named proposal developers were resolved as H0002: Turner’s authored advocacy is A; four co-developers are B for explicit third-party attribution, not generic membership. Current union policy and the additional Māori/NZSL, cultural and funder layers remain follow-up priorities.

The wealthy/business sweep found separate interview evidence for Malcolm Rands and Phillip Mills; it did not import the other generic letter signers. Rands accepts a wealth tax among several options. Mills's original Stuff interview was read through Jina; an accessible reproduction in [Covisory Connect](https://www.covisory.com/content/CONNECT_Q2_29.6.2023_INT.pdf) supplies the checked citation. A Ben Kepes LinkedIn article remained a lead because full-source access failed.

[Ipsos's September 2026 report](https://www.ipsos.com/en-nz/34th-ipsos-nz-issues-monitor-sept-2026) records 64% support and 26% opposition among 1,003 adults, fieldwork 3–11 September. The precise tested design is a 2.5% tax on individual net assets above NZ$10m, family homes exempt. It is stored separately from the supporter register. The broader Vote Compass question about wealthy people paying more is not a wealth-tax-design poll and was not substituted for it.

The [Public Health Advisory Committee's 2025 report](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2025-08/PHAC-determining-our-future-v5.pdf) uses 'wealth tax levers' in its summary, but its explanatory text gives capital-gains tax as the concrete example; the committee and its members were not added. Searches of local officeholders also returned property rates described as wealth taxes and satire: neither qualifies.

R002 source checks and independent meaning/grade review are recorded in the run log. The fuzzy duplicate candidates are false positives: the Greens and Progressive Party are distinct parties, and Grant Robertson (former minister) and Graham Robertson (business advocate) are distinct people. Root placements for Shaw, Parker and Robertson reflect former list roles; no constituency was invented.
