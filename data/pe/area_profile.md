# Area profile — Peru

## Scope
Seed in progress, national actors with regional and local research outstanding. Search Spanish; Quechua and Aymara searches remain outstanding. The district geography is an INEI-based 2026 compilation, which includes reconstructed newer districts; check against the official register. [Source](https://github.com/Rodasluis/Peru-maps).

## Debate and sources
In 2020 the Frente Amplio sponsored an annual net wealth tax above 400 UIT ([Congress](https://comunicaciones.congreso.gob.pe/despacho-informa/congresistas-del-fa-presentan-proyecto-de-ley-de-impuesto-a-las-grandes-fortunas/)). In 2020 Somos Perú proposed a progressive wealth tax ([Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-congreso-somos-peru-plantea-crear-impuesto-sobre-riqueza-820568.aspx)). The 2022 Portalatino bill proposed taxation above 3,500 UIT ([bill](https://img.lpderecho.pe/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Proyecto-de-ley-3060-2022-CR-LPDerecho.pdf)). Congressional bill status and cosponsors remain to be investigated.

## Geography
Departments, provinces and districts are represented. Official boundaries are recorded by [RENLIM](https://www.gob.pe/98535-acceder-al-registro-nacional-de-limites-renlim); the [INEI portal](https://ide.inei.gob.pe/) publishes district layers. An open licence and matching codes have not been verified, so no map is included. No local sample has been completed.

## Further research — R002
The [Venceremos 2026–2031 programme](https://mpesije.jne.gob.pe/docs/9984addc-e998-43b1-920f-1178d4d973aa.pdf) calls for a tax on fortunes above US$100 million. The [CGTP May Day statement](https://www.cgtp.org.pe/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Labor16.pdf) demands a tax on large fortunes. A secondary [bill index](https://jeqo.github.io/peru-congreso/proyectos-ley/2016-2021/06600/06615/) names further sponsors of bill 06615, but the official document was blocked and signatures need checking. Bill 4887 needs tax-base review: reporting suggests annual turnover, which would fall outside the definition. Local government sampling, the official district-list comparison, and Quechua and Aymara searches remain outstanding.

A broad web sample for Lima, Arequipa, Cusco, Trujillo and Piura returned no confirmed local-government supporters; it did not inspect council records, so these remain pending. The [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/es/publications/politicas-de-desarrollo-regional-en-peru_3adc5cee-es/full-report/subnational-institutional-and-fiscal-capacity_da451c57.html) describes local taxes as nationally rate-set and regional governments as lacking taxing power. The PST party statement could not be opened (HTTP 500) and remains a lead.

## R003: Party programmes
[Juntos por el Perú’s 2021 plan](https://bitacora.jomra.es/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/pg21_juntosporelperu.pdf) calls for a wealth tax, and its [2026 plan](https://mpesije.jne.gob.pe/docs/3dd0e649-061c-4f31-8c3f-7a0836b58bde.pdf) calls for one during a crisis. The [Partido Morado 2026 programme](https://www.elmalmenor.com/docs/planes/Partido_Morado.pdf) specifies 1% on wealth exceeding US$5 million. These programmes support the organisations; they do not by themselves establish individual candidate positions. The official open-data portal for district codes returned HTTP 403; the independently compiled geography has not yet been checked against the official list.
