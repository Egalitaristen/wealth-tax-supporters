# Australia: area profile

**Scope.** Initial national research only; regional and local research and the local government geography and map remain to be completed. Australia uses the `australia` folder because `au` is occupied by the African Union.

**Sources opened.** The Australian Greens proposed an annual billionaires tax in [2025](https://greens.org.au/news/greens-will-tax-150-billionaires-part-revenue-plan-fund-dental-medicare-gp-free-and-other-cost); [Oxfam Australia](https://www.oxfam.org.au/media-releases/wealth-of-three-richest-australians-has-doubled-since-2020-as-cost-of-living-pressures-for-many-continue-to-bite-oxfam/) called for a progressive wealth tax in 2024; [The Australia Institute](https://flosse.dss.gov.au/entities/publication/25f92749-4ce5-4911-bb2a-5b2cffed7b8f) proposed an annual wealth tax in 2025.

**Next.** Load official local government geography and openly licensed boundaries, then survey parliamentary records, individual politicians, unions, economists, business, regional governments and a sample of local councils. No layer is exhausted.

**Geography.** The [Australian Bureau of Statistics 2025 LGA boundaries](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/standards/australian-statistical-geography-standard-asgs/edition-3-july-2021-june-2026/access-and-downloads/digital-boundary-files) supply 548 mapped areas, including unincorporated areas that are not councils; 2021 state and territory boundaries supply outlines. Both are CC BY 4.0. ABS areas without geometry (including no usual address and offshore categories) are excluded. Geography has not been researched for supporters.

**Checks and gaps.** Six quotations were read on their source pages; automated checkquotes found three and skipped three when retrieval failed. The ACTU 2012 Buffett Rule proposal was excluded as an income-tax proposal. Federal Parliament, other parties, unions, public wealthy supporters, faith, regional assemblies and local councils need further work; no local sample or parliamentary seat roster has been completed. The first priority is direct statements by federal politicians and public wealthy supporters, followed by unions and civil society, then regional and five-city local samples.
