## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- Estonia has a land tax (maamaks) and, since 2025, a security tax and car tax, but no domestic net-wealth tax.
- 2019-11-19: At the Riigikogu debate "Maksusüsteem – kuidas edasi?", entrepreneur and former Hansapank CEO Indrek Neivelt explicitly proposed an annual net wealth tax of 1% (or 0.5%) on assets exceeding 10 million euros, drawing on Elizabeth Warren's wealth tax proposal (recorded, grade A; published in Postimees).
- 2019-11-22: Economic cyberneticist and former aviation executive Toomas Peterson published an op-ed on ERR calling for the introduction of a capital or wealth tax ("rikkusemaks") to curb wealth concentration (recorded, grade B).
- 2021-2022: Arenguseire Keskus (Foresight Centre) modeled tax scenarios including property and asset taxes ("Varamaksud Eestis ja Euroopa riikides" by Magnus Piirits); excluded as scenario modeling focused on property tax rather than personal advocacy for a net-wealth tax.
- 2023-05-10: European Parliament vote on A9-0155/2023 Amendment 2 (EU own resource based on progressive wealth tax): Jana Toom voted FOR (grade A).
- 2023-09-14: SDE chair Lauri Läänemets stated "asset taxes, taxes on wealthy, rich people or businesses - there's no escaping it" (ERR); his concrete proposals were a progressive income tax and company asset tax (recorded, grade C).
- 2023-11-30: Eesti Vasakliit (Estonian Left Party, formerly EÜVP) adopted section 9.3 of its general programme, pledging to introduce a progressive wealth tax ("astmeline varamaks") on the wealthiest segment of society (recorded, grade A).
- 2023-12-12: European Parliament roll-call vote 62 on A9-0336/2023 Amendment 2 (solidarity levies on high wealth and global wealth tax negotiations): Marina Kaljurand (SDE), Sven Mikser (SDE), and Jana Toom (Centre) voted FOR (recorded, grade A).
- 2024-04-20: Erakond Eestimaa Rohelised (Estonian Greens) officially adopted an EU unified wealth tax ("ELi ühtne varamaks") in its 2024 European election priorities platform (recorded, grade A).
- 2024-04: Kerly Randlane (TUUM Consulting) published an op-ed in ERR advocating wealth taxes ("varamaksud") in place of a progressive income tax (recorded, grade B).
- 2024-07-26: Political scientist and former Supreme Council member Peet Kask (University of Tartu) published an op-ed in ERR proposing a progressive pure wealth tax ("puhtakujuline varandusmaks") on the top 1% exempting lower assets, modeled on Spain, France, Norway, Switzerland and Saez-Zucman, to generate ~€630m annually (recorded, grade A).
- 2024-10-16: Professor Rainer Kattel (UCL IIPP / former TalTech) argued in Müürileht that inheritance and wealth taxes ("varamaksud") increase economic competition and reduce inequality (recorded, grade B).
- 2024–2026: Employers and business associations discussed an "ettevõtete varamaks" (company asset tax); excluded as a corporate tax. Trade unions (EAKL, Jaan-Hendrik Toomel) called for "varamaksud" explicitly defined as real estate and luxury goods taxes; excluded as out of definition. Youth wing figures (e.g. Ako Monticelli, Noorsotsid) highlight wealth inequality but propose progressive income tax (astmeline tulumaks).

## 3. Legislatures
- Riigikogu: 101 seats, open API api.riigikogu.ee. No domestic personal net-wealth tax bills introduced; tax proposals by SDE and Keskerakond focus on progressive income taxes (astmeline tulumaks) and corporate taxation.
- European Parliament: Estonian delegation consists of 7 MEPs. Roll-call votes 129 (2023-05-10) and 62 (2023-12-12) recorded.
