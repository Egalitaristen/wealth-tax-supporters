A wealth tax is charged above a line, and the line is a number in a statute. Who pays is decided by that number and by nothing else. So the claim that the tax will spread to the middle class is a claim about what happens to the number. The record answers it. Sweden, Denmark, Spain, France and Norway each raised their line, and Norway also froze its line in some years, which tightened it. Where the line sank in real terms, it was because it stood still while prices rose, and the fix is a clause.

The tax on the table now is aimed far above anything a family owns. A minimum tax on billionaires starts at $1 billion and reaches about 3,000 households in the world.[15] That line is 5,184 times the net worth of the typical American family.[13] No gradual slide closes a gap that size. Reaching the middle class would take a different law, a new vote and a rewritten standard, and the middle class would see every step.

The pressure on wealth taxes has run the other way. Twelve OECD countries levied an annual tax on individual net wealth in 1990 and four did in 2017.[4] Income tax did spread, once, for reasons that do not apply here. The rest of this page shows each step.

## What parliaments did with the line {#thresholds}

**Sweden.** The threshold started at SEK 800,000 in 1991 and reached SEK 3 million in 2006. In 1999 every household above the 92nd percentile of wealth owed the tax. In 2006 singles still owed it above the 92nd percentile, but a couple had to be in the top 2%.[5]

**Denmark.** The law set a base allowance of DKK 1,278,700 and said allowances were adjusted by a separate rule. By 1996 the allowance stood at DKK 1,635,500. The government's bill that year proposed raising it to DKK 2.4 million, cutting the rate from 1% to 0.7% and ending the tax from 1997. The tax reached an estimated 35,000 families.[6]

**Spain.** Before 2008 the exemption was between EUR 108,182 and EUR 150,000, depending on the region. When Spain brought the tax back in 2011 it set the national exemption at EUR 700,000. The number of taxpayers fell from 981,498 in 2007 to 188,680 in 2015.[4]

**France.** The exemption went from EUR 800,000 to EUR 1.3 million in 2012. In 2016 the tax reached 351,152 households, slightly under 1% of all tax households.[4] The OECD records why governments do this: increases in thresholds were often motivated by the wish not to burden the middle or upper-middle class as asset values, housing most of all, rose.[4]

**Norway.** The line for one person was NOK 120,000 in 2000 and NOK 1.7 million in 2022. For 2026 parliament set it at NOK 1.9 million, 15.8 times the 2000 figure.[2][3]

{{chart: norway-share}}

In 2001, 37.7% of Norwegian adults paid the tax. By 2016, as the line rose, 11.6% did.[1] Then the share rose again to 15.4% in 2024, and that is the part the objection points at. Look at what happened. Parliament raised the line in 2019 and 2022 and froze it in nominal terms in 2018, 2023 and 2024. The research service of the Storting itself calls a freeze a tightening.[2] So every step was a vote, in the open, with a revenue estimate attached. The people it brought in pay little: of the 701,482 who paid in 2024, 70% paid under NOK 25,000.[1] Norway's line has always been low compared with other countries.[4] No one proposes that design for a billionaire minimum tax, and the next section shows how to stop a line from drifting.

## A line drifts down only when nobody indexes it {#erosion}

The only way a threshold sinks without a vote is inflation on a fixed number. A tax with a fixed line pulls in more households as prices and asset values rise. That is a defect in the drafting, and the cure is old. Uruguay's law has said since 1996 that the government sets the tax-free minimum every year by the change in the cost of living. For 2024 it rose 5.32%, to UYU 6,381,000.[7][8] Colombia writes its scale in a unit, the UVT, that the tax authority resets every year by the change in consumer prices.[21] The OECD's advice to every country is that the threshold should be revised annually or every few years to account for inflation.[4]

Colombia shows which way the pressure goes once the line is defended. The line stands at 72,000 UVT. In October 2026 the government filed a bill that would raise the line to 95,500 UVT for 2027 to 2030 only, so that some people who pay today would stop.[10] In 2025 34,229 people paid it, and those above 239,000 UVT paid 72% of the revenue.[11] The bill has not passed. It is the government proposing to shrink the group.

## A minimum tax sits far from the middle {#minimum}

The billionaire minimum tax is set in dollars. Anyone worth more than $1 billion pays at least 2% of their wealth a year, in whatever mix of taxes their country uses, and someone who already pays that owes nothing extra.[15] About 3,000 households are in that group, the richest one in a million.[15]

{{chart: lines-vs-median}}

The typical American family had a net worth of $192,900 in 2022.[13] Divide: Senator Warren's 2019 wealth tax, which starts at $50 million, sits 259 times above it. A minimum tax from $100 million sits 518 times above it. The $1 billion line sits 5,184 times above it. Less than 0.1% of American families would owe the Warren tax.[14] Even the widest design on the table starts hundreds of times above a family with a paid-off house and a pension. A sliding line would have to cross that whole gap, and a floor that several countries adopt together would have to be reopened by all of them.

## Income tax did spread, and the reason does not carry over {#income}

The income tax is the comparison to make, and the American one is documented. It began in 1913 and 357,598 returns were filed for that year.[16] In 1916 the law exempted the first $3,000 of a single person's income and $4,000 for a married person's.[16] By 1918, 4,425,114 returns were filed, 12.4 times the 1913 number, and 1,516,938 of them, 34%, reported net incomes of $1,000 to $2,000.[18][16] The years between were the years of the First World War, and Congress passed new revenue acts in 1917 and 1918.[17][18]

{{chart: income-returns}}

Two things make a wealth tax a different case. First, income is something nearly every household has, so lowering a line on income reaches millions at once. Wealth at the top is held by a few, 3,000 households for the billionaire minimum, and there is no mass of taxpayers hidden below the line.[15] Second, the income tax's exemption was an ordinary number in an ordinary statute with no index and no named group of payers. By 1918, 34% of all returns reported incomes of $1,000 to $2,000, well below the $3,000 line of 1916.[18][16] Nothing guarded the line, and so it moved.

## Write the line where it is hard to move {#design}

- **Put the number in the statute, in the currency of the tax.** Colombia does this, and a change takes an act of Congress.[10]
- **Index it.** Prices, as in Uruguay, or median wealth, so that the line never drifts toward the middle.[7][4]
- **Name who the tax is for.** The G20 report prices the minimum tax at $1 billion for about 3,000 households, and a version at $100 million.[15] A proposal that says who pays has a number to be tested against.
- **Charge the difference, not the whole.** A minimum tax collects only what a fortune pays below the floor. Someone who already pays the floor owes nothing, so lowering the line does not tax the middle class more.[15]

If a wealth tax reaches the household with millions, as Norway's does, it is because that household has the means and the tax was drafted that way. That is a different design from the one proposed for billionaires, and it was chosen by votes.

## The pressure has run the other way {#pressure}

Argentina's 2024 law cuts the top rate of its tax from 1.5% in 2023 to a flat 0.25% in 2027.[12] Uruguay's law cut its rates every year to a floor of 0.1%, and the tax on individuals now raises 0.01% of GDP, against 0.6% in the 1990s.[9] In February 2026 Høyre's national assembly voted 158 to 131 to abolish Norway's tax.[19] The register counts 10 countries with a wealth tax and 13 that used to have one, as of 13 November 2026.[20] Wealth taxes lose ground. The pages on [Europe repealing the tax](/arguments/europe-repealed-it) and on [why it is needed at all](/arguments/why-tax-wealth) say what that cost. A slide toward the middle class would need a trend that does not exist.
