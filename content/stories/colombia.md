In 2025, 34,229 Colombians paid a wealth tax and it raised COP 1.22 trillion, about US$325 million.[5][3] The richest paid most of it: fortunes above 239,000 tax units, COP 11.9 billion or about US$3.2 million in 2025, paid COP 878 billion, 72% of the total.[5] Below 72,000 units, COP 3.77 billion or about US$1.0 million in 2026, nobody pays anything.[1][2] Dollar figures here use COP 3,757 per dollar, the rate on 31 December 2025.[3]

Why tax wealth when Colombia already taxes income? Because the biggest fortunes grow without ever showing up as income. A share that doubles, a company that keeps its profit, a building that gains value: none of it is taxed until it is sold, and the owner can borrow against it in the meantime. The [pillar page](/arguments/why-tax-wealth#unrealised) follows the money step by step. José Antonio Ocampo, the finance minister who wrote the 2022 reform, said that the worst-distributed thing in Colombia is wealth.[9]

That is the tax the new government wants to shrink. President Abelardo de la Espriella said in August 2026 that it would be eliminated.[6] The bill his government filed on 29 October does not do that. It keeps the tax for individuals in 2027 to 2030, lifts the line to 95,500 units, and has not passed: on 10 November the joint economic committees were still naming the rapporteurs for its first debate.[12][13][15] The law of 2022 had already set the 1.5% top rate, the one that drew most of that 72%, to end after 2026.[1]

## How the tax works {#design}

The tax is charged once a year on net wealth, assets minus debts, held on 1 January. It applies to individuals and estates that file income tax, and to non-residents on what they hold in Colombia. Foreign entities pay on property they own here such as real estate, yachts, aircraft and mining rights. Their shares and portfolio investments are left out.[1]

The scale is written in tax units, the UVT, which the tax authority DIAN resets every year. For 2026 one unit is COP 52,374.[2] The first 12,000 units of the home where you live are excluded, COP 628 million or about US$167,000. Second and holiday homes get no exclusion.[1][2] The rates apply band by band:[1]

- Up to 72,000 UVT: nothing.
- From 72,000 to 122,000 UVT: 0.5%.
- From 122,000 to 239,000 UVT: 1%, on top of 250 UVT.
- Above 239,000 UVT: 1.5%, on top of 1,420 UVT.

{{chart: bands}}

Take a fortune of 300,000 units after debts and the home exclusion, COP 15.7 billion or US$4.2 million. It owes 2,335 units, COP 122 million, about US$33,000, which is 0.78% of the fortune a year.[1][2][3] The [calculator](/calculator?w=15712200000&b=3770928000:0.5,6389628000:1,12517386000:1.5&c=COP$) has the 2026 scale in pesos and gives the same result. The page on [thresholds and spreading](/arguments/it-will-spread) shows why a scale written in a unit that follows prices keeps the line where it is.

## What it raises {#revenue}

Spread over the people who paid, the tax is small: COP 1.22 trillion from 34,229 payers is about COP 36 million each, roughly US$9,500 a year.[5][3] The weight sits at the top. Wealth above COP 11.9 billion paid COP 878 billion, 72% of everything.[5] So the top rate carries the tax.

In 2024 the tax raised COP 1.47 trillion, 0.55% of everything DIAN collected, and in 2025 COP 1.22 trillion, 0.40%.[6][5] Earlier wealth taxes raised much more. The record year was 2015, with COP 5.51 trillion, and 2016 followed with 5.06 trillion, while 2022 was the weakest of the 20 years, with COP 73 billion.[4]

Companies show what the base can bear. In 2026 the government used an economic emergency to charge an extraordinary wealth tax on companies with equity of 200,000 units or more, COP 10.5 billion. The general rate was 0.5%, and 1.6% for banks, insurers and the coal and oil industries.[11] It raised COP 5.1 trillion in two instalments, April and May 2026, more than four times what the tax on individuals raised in all of 2025.[10][5] The government had aimed at COP 8.3 trillion. César Cermeño of the Universidad de los Andes attributes the shortfall to the separate 1.6% rate for the financial and extraction sectors, which he says reduced the base collected from them.[10] A rate set by decree is a rate the next decree can change.

## How the tax came back {#history}

DIAN's series starts in 2006 with a rate of 0.3% on net equity above COP 3 billion. Over the 20 years since, the wealth taxes have collected a little over COP 49 trillion.[4]

In 2022 the new government proposed a permanent tax. Ocampo's bill started at COP 3 billion, with 0.5% up to COP 5 billion and 1% above, and counted on COP 2.6 trillion a year.[9] Congress added the third rate. On 3 November 2022 the Chamber of Representatives passed the block of articles that included the tax, 136 votes to 35, and Ley 2277 became law on 13 December.[23][1]

Support goes back at least to 2020. On 13 May 2020, 50 senators asked to expand the wealth tax to pay for the pandemic response. They came from nine groupings: 14 from the Partido de la U and 12 Liberals among them, with Alianza Verde, the Polo Democrático and others. [Gustavo Petro](/colombia/A0014) and [Iván Cepeda](/colombia/A0008) signed.[21]

## The top rate is scheduled to end {#leaks}

The 1.5% band, the one that drew 72% of the revenue, is written into the law for 2023 to 2026 only. From 2027 the table stops at 1% above 122,000 units.[1] The same fortune of 300,000 units would owe 2,030 units instead of 2,335, about US$28,000 instead of US$33,000.[1][2][3] Nobody has to vote to cut the tax on the richest: the end date does it. The fix is one clause.

The other gaps are in what is left out. A second home or a holiday home gets no exclusion, and shares of foreign entities that own property here are not counted.[1]

## What the opponents say {#objections}

Bruce Mac Master, the head of the business association ANDI, says the tax has hit investment hard and that some capital and some people have moved their tax residence.[18] The only numbers he gave were about growth, 1.7% in 2024 and 2.6% in 2025, not about the tax.[18] On the same question the pages on [hurts growth](/arguments/hurts-growth) and [they will leave](/arguments/they-will-leave) set out what the studies of Norway and other countries find.

The economist Luis Fernando Mejía says a 1.5% tax on an asset that earns 3% a year takes half of its income.[19] That sum treats a tax on a fortune as if it were a tax on one year's income. The fortune at the top rarely shows its growth as income, as the [pillar page](/arguments/why-tax-wealth#unrealised) explains, and the 1.5% rate is the one the law ends after 2026. The pillar page's section on [a lower rate on a wider base](/arguments/why-tax-wealth#lower-rate) shows the design that answers him.

Owners with no cash are met in the page on [wealth you cannot sell](/arguments/no-cash), family companies in [family firms](/arguments/family-firms), and how a tax values what it counts in [you cannot value it](/arguments/you-cannot-value-it).

## The courts {#courts}

The Constitutional Court looked at the tax twice and left it standing. On 19 September 2024, in sentence C-395, it declined to decide a challenge to articles 35 to 41 of the law, finding that the claims were not argued well enough. On 4 February 2025, in sentence C-031, it did the same with two joined challenges. It said they ignored the threshold and the rising rates that soften the tax.[7][8] The page on [constitutional challenges](/arguments/unconstitutional) puts Colombia beside Germany, France, Spain and Switzerland.

## The politics now {#now}

Colombia voted on 21 June 2026. Iván Cepeda, who signed the 2020 request to expand the tax, lost the runoff to Abelardo de la Espriella, 48.7% to 49.7% in the first count.[21][22] De la Espriella pledged to lower taxes.[22] In his inauguration speech in August he said the wealth tax would be eliminated, from 1 January 2027.[6]

The outgoing government had gone the other way. On 20 July 2026 its Finance Minister, Germán Ávila, filed a reform, bill 004, that would lower the line from 72,000 to 40,000 units.[16][17] It sits in the Chamber's third committee, and its rapporteur's report recommends rejecting it.[16]

The new government's bill is number 477 of 2026 in the Chamber, the Ley de Rescate Económico, filed on 29 October.[12][14] For individuals it keeps the tax for 2027 to 2030 and raises the line from 72,000 to 95,500 units, COP 5.0 billion at the 2026 unit and about US$1.3 million. Rates would be 0.5% from 95,500 to 145,500 units and 1% above.[12][13][2][3] The homes exclusion stays as it is.[13] The same fortune of 300,000 units would owe 1,795 units, 235 units less than under the law as it stands for 2027, and everyone between 72,000 and 95,500 units would owe nothing.

{{chart: owed}}

On 3 November the president sent a message of urgency, which sets a 30-day limit on each chamber and gives the bill priority. It does not make the bill law.[14] The schedule reported on 10 November put its first debate, in the joint economic committees of both chambers, in the last week of November, and the session ends in the week of 14 to 17 December.[15] Until a law is published, the 2022 law stands.

In a column in August, Ocampo asked the president not to repeal the tax, which he called the most progressive of all taxes, and said it could help pay for rebuilding after the earthquake of 10 August.[20] Senator [Kevin Gómez Paz](/colombia/A0006) says repeal gives relief to those who need it least.[19]

## Who supports it {#who-supports}

The tax has a base across parties. The 2020 request to expand it came from senators of the Partido de la U, the Liberals, Alianza Verde, the Polo Democrático, Cambio Radical and Colombia Humana.[21] José Antonio Ocampo wrote the 2022 reform and defends it now.[9][20] The economist [Leopoldo Fergusson](/colombia/A0059) called its design good, even generous, because it starts at COP 3 billion, and with Marc Hofstetter had earlier proposed a lower start, COP 1 billion with no deductions.[9] Senator [Kevin Gómez Paz](/colombia/A0006) argues against repeal.[19] The pillar page lists [who backs the tax around the world](/arguments/why-tax-wealth#who).

## What Colombia shows {#lessons}

### A line that follows prices {#threshold}

The scale is in tax units, so the pesos move with DIAN's unit each year.[2] This site argues for the lower start that Fergusson and Hofstetter proposed: COP 1 billion, about US$270,000.[9][3]

### Revenue sits at the top {#top}

Wealth above 239,000 units paid 72% of the 2025 revenue.[5] This site argues for removing the end date of the 1.5% band, so that the tax on the largest fortunes stays where Congress put it.

### Repeal needs a vote {#repeal}

The 2022 law ended the cycle of temporary versions. Changing it takes an act of Congress, and bill 477 has not passed.[12][15] This site argues against both changes in it: the end date of 2030 and the line at 95,500 units.

### The neighbours {#neighbours}

Argentina has taxed personal assets since 1991 and is cutting the tax; the [Argentine story](/argentina) follows that case. [Bolivia](/bolivia) has its own tax on large fortunes. The page on [Europe repealing the tax](/arguments/europe-repealed-it) shows what repeal cost elsewhere.
