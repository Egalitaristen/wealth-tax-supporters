Spain taxes wealth twice. The old wealth tax is run by the regions, and each can set its own minimum and rates, or rebate the whole bill.[1][10] In 2022 a new state tax on net wealth above €3 million set a floor under it, so that no region can let the biggest fortunes off.[2] In 2024 the two raised €2.2 billion from about 185,000 payers, outside the Basque Country and Navarre, which collect their own.[8][33]

## The wealth tax {#wealth-tax}

The tax dates from 1977.[3] Each year you add up what you own at 31 December and subtract your debts.[1] A main home is exempt up to €300,000. So are pension rights, business assets and shares in active companies that meet the conditions in the law.[1] Where a region sets nothing, the first €700,000 is untaxed and the rest meets a scale from 0.2% to 3.5%, the top rate applying to the part above €10.7 million.[1] A cap protects against overload: wealth tax and income tax together cannot take more than 60% of a person's income-tax base.[1]

In 2008 the government left the tax on the books but gave every taxpayer a 100% rebate and ended the duty to file.[3][32] Its own 2011 decree says the tax had come to hit mostly middle-sized fortunes.[4] The tax came back for 2011 and 2012 with the €700,000 minimum and the €300,000 home exemption.[4]

## The solidarity tax {#solidarity}

A law of 27 December 2022 created the solidarity tax.[2] It covers anyone with net wealth above €3 million and exempts the same things as the wealth tax. The base is net wealth less €700,000, and the first €3 million of base carries a 0% rate, so the first euro of tax falls at €3.7 million. Above that the rates are 1.7%, 2.1% and 3.5%, the last above a base of €10.7 million.[2] Wealth tax already paid comes off the bill. The law gives two aims: raise money, and keep the burden on residents of regions that cut the wealth tax close to the burden elsewhere.[2]

In December 2023 the government extended the tax past its first two years, until wealth taxation is reviewed in the reform of regional financing.[5] Madrid, Andalusia and La Rioja used to rebate all of their wealth tax. Now they rebate only the amount by which the wealth-tax bill exceeds the solidarity bill, and Cantabria and Murcia follow the same rule.[10]

Here is what that does. Take €20 million of net wealth with no exempt assets. The solidarity bill is about €453,000. A Madrid resident pays that sum in all. A Catalan resident owes about €435,000 of wealth tax on the Catalan scale and about €18,000 more on the solidarity return, which also totals about €453,000.[2][10][35] At €5 million the Madrid resident pays €22,000 and the Catalan about €60,000.[2][10][35] Madrid keeps a discount for the middle fortunes. The floor takes it away for the biggest ones.

## What it raised {#revenue}

On 20 September 2023 the finance ministry reported that the first solidarity returns came from about 12,000 payers, who paid €620 million. Madrid supplied about 10,300 of them and €555 million.[6] For 2024 the tax agency counts about 227,000 wealth-tax returns and €2.2 billion payable under the two taxes, up 11% from €1.96 billion in 2023.[8][9] Since the rebates changed, most of the money arrives through the wealth-tax return. The agency's wealth-tax summary shows €2.15 billion for 2024, leaving about €24 million for the solidarity returns.[7][8]

## The regions undercut each other {#competition}

Where a region rebates the tax as Madrid does, only people above the solidarity threshold pay anything. Catalonia holds about 95,000 of the 227,000 returns, 42%, and 97% of its filers pay. It raised €768 million. Madrid holds about 25,500 returns, 11%, and 45% of its filers pay, about 11,400 people paying an average of €57,000 each, for €650 million.[8]

Undercutting costs more than the tax itself. When the tax returned in 2011, every region except Madrid levied it. The moves of wealthy people to Madrid cost the regions that lost them six times as much income-tax revenue as the direct wealth-tax loss. Researchers found that Madrid itself could raise more by agreeing to a common positive rate.[27] The floor pushes in that direction. In 2023 Madrid lost wealthy taxpayers on net for the first time since 2016. The same study proposes extending the minimum tax to wealth between €700,000 and €3.7 million.[28] We argue for a state floor that starts lower.

## The courts {#courts}

Madrid's government, Andalusia's, Galicia's and the Madrid Assembly each asked the Constitutional Court to strike the tax down, and each lost. On 7 November 2023 the court dismissed Madrid's appeal in full. It rejected every claim, from the way the tax was passed to ability to pay and legal certainty, and held that the tax does not interfere with the regions' powers over the wealth tax. Four of the eleven judges dissented.[11] The other three appeals fell on 22 November and 12 December, each time citing the first ruling.[12][13][14]

## The Basque Country and Navarre {#foral}

The Basque territories collect their own taxes, and a law of 3 April 2023 let each of them write its own solidarity tax.[34] Álava passed its version on 15 December 2023, Gipuzkoa on 28 December, and Bizkaia published its on 26 December.[15][16][18] Álava and Gipuzkoa share one scale: 0% on the first €3.2 million of base, 1.7% from there, 2.1% from €6.4 million, 2.5% from €12.8 million and 3.5% above €14.5 million.[15][16] Bizkaia's scale is lower below that last band, at 1.5% from €3.2 million, 1.75% from €6.4 million and 2% from €12.8 million, with an €800,000 minimum.[18] Navarre added a 3.5% bracket to its wealth-tax scale above €11 million for 2022 and 2023.[17]

## The political fight {#politics}

In June 2022 Congress refused to consider the Unidas Podemos bill for a tax on fortunes above €10 million, with the Socialists voting no.[19] The Socialist and Unidas Podemos groups then put the solidarity tax through as an amendment, and it passed that December.[2][11] The People's Party's 2023 programme promises to abolish it.[20]

The PP's own position has shifted since. On 25 May 2026 the Finance Committee spokespeople of the PP, PSOE and Sumar all said, at an Oxfam Intermón conference, that something like the Zucman tax is needed in Spain. The report added that the PP would favour a tax on the very large fortunes created by technology and giant companies. For the PP, [Pedro Puy](/es/A0112) called the wealth tax complex and said other mechanisms could be more effective.[21]

On 30 June 2025, at a conference in Seville, Pedro Sánchez asked for international taxation that guarantees fair taxation of large fortunes.[24] On 6 February 2026 Minister Pablo Bustinduy met the economist Gabriel Zucman in Paris to study a 2% tax on wealth above €100 million.[25] On 16 September 2026 Sumar filed a bill for a 3% levy on net wealth above €50 million, and the Congress board admitted it on 22 September.[22] The Cortes were dissolved on 5 October and Spain votes on 29 November 2026.[23]

## Who supports it {#who-supports}

In August 2022, 71% of 1,078 adults polled by Simple Lógica backed the tax on fortunes above €10 million.[29] Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](/es/A0062) argues for taxing large fortunes internationally, and Minister [Pablo Bustinduy](/es/A0064) wants Spain to adopt the 2% minimum of [Gabriel Zucman](/es/A0084).[24][25] [Carlos Martín Urriza](/es/A0110) leads Sumar on the Finance Committee and signed the 3% bill.[22] The economist [Jesús Ruiz-Huerta](/es/A0079) chaired the expert committee whose 2022 report recommended keeping the wealth tax and named the race to the bottom among regions as a problem.[31] [Oxfam Intermón](/es/A0067) published the 2026 report that proposes a 3% minimum above €50 million.[30]

## What Spain shows {#lessons}

### Valuation {#valuation}

The largest hole in Spain's tax is exemption. In 2024 €435 billion of the €987 billion of assets declared, 44%, was exempt, and €374 billion of that was unlisted shares in exempt companies.[7] The scales produced €6.2 billion. The income-tax cap removed €3.1 billion, and €2.2 billion remained payable after the regional rebates.[7] Oxfam Intermón estimates that 8 of every 10 euros of potential revenue are lost to gaps in the design.[30] Catalan returns for 2011 to 2015 show where the money goes. After a rise of 0.1 percentage point in the average rate, taxable wealth fell 3.2% over four years. Savings did not fall. Taxpayers moved money into exempt business assets and used the income-tax cap.[26] The Sumar bill of 2026 drops the general exemption for business assets and shares, and we argue for dropping it from the wealth tax too.[22]

### Flight {#flight}

The Spanish evidence shows wealthy people moving between regions, and no study we found measures departures from Spain itself. The floor already narrows the gap between regions. With no exempt assets, a resident of Madrid and a resident of Catalonia owe the same from about €17.5 million of net wealth.[2][10][35] Below that the Catalan pays more, as the €5 million case shows. A floor that starts at €700,000 would end the gain from moving for the rest.

### Revenue {#revenue-lesson}

Combined revenue rose from €1.4 billion in 2021 to €2.2 billion in 2024.[28][8] Simulations at the World Inequality Lab put it at nearly €5 billion, more than twice the 2023 level, if the business-asset exemptions were removed.[28]
